\begin{abstract}
An adversarial review of \texttt{mintime\_second\_order\_audit.tex} (2026-09-10)
found that our statement of the sufficiency theorem was ``not a complete,
usable statement'': a citation, not a mapping. This document is the mapping.
It states the theorem with its hypotheses and its conclusion in full, gives
the dictionary from each hypothesis to a quantity we compute, and says
plainly which hypotheses are \emph{proved}, which are \emph{measured at
samples}, and which are \emph{assumed}. It is deliberately short, and it is
the document a methods section should quote rather than the audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}
Minimum time for the controlled CR3BP with depleting mass. State
$x=(r,v,m)\in\R^7$, control a thrust direction $\alpha\in S^2$ and a throttle
$s\in[0,1]$:
\[
  \dot r=v,\qquad \dot v=g(r)+h(v)+s\frac{T}{m}\alpha,\qquad \dot m=-s\frac{T}{c}.
\]
Endpoints: $x(0)=(r_0,v_0,1)$ fixed, $(r,v)(\tf)=(r_f,v_f)$ fixed, $m(\tf)$
free, $\tf$ free. Cost $J=\tf$.

\section{Reduction to the theorem's setting}
The theorem addresses a \emph{smooth} control problem with a point target and
free final time. Two reductions get us there, and the second is the one that
was previously mis-attributed.

\paragraph{R1 --- the throttle is eliminated by STRICT BANG, not by counting
competitors.} The Hamiltonian is \emph{affine} in $s$,
\[
  H(s)=1+\lr\!\cdot\!v+\lv\!\cdot\!(g+h)-s\,T\,Q_{mt},
  \qquad Q_{mt}=\frac{|\lv|}{m}+\frac{\lm}{c},
\]
so $Q_{mt}>0$ strictly makes $s^{*}=1$ the \emph{unique} minimiser with a
margin at every time. A strictly bang component may then be eliminated,
leaving a smooth problem in $\alpha$ alone. \emph{The earlier argument, that
the all-burn competitor set equals the full one, is false and is withdrawn:
it does not, and $\delta m_f$ really does acquire a throttle term.}

\paragraph{R2 --- the mass is then a known function of time.} With $s\equiv1$,
$m(t)=1-Tt/c$ identically, so the $(r,v)$ dynamics close on themselves as a
\emph{non-autonomous} 6-state system, and the target is a point in $\R^6$.

\section{The theorem}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{(Bonnard--Caillau--Tr\'elat 2007; Agrachev--Sachkov \S21.3.)}
Let $(x(\cdot),\alpha(\cdot))$ be a \emph{normal} extremal of the smooth
free-final-time problem with a point target, on $[0,\tf]$. Suppose
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(H-a)}] the strengthened Legendre condition holds along the arc:
  $H_{\alpha\alpha}$ restricted to $T_\alpha S^2$ is positive definite;
\item[\textbf{(H-b)}] there is no conjugate time in $(0,\tf]$.
\end{itemize}
Then the trajectory is a \emph{strict strong} local minimiser --- minimal in
the $C^0$ topology, among trajectories with the same endpoints --- and the
minimising control is uniquely determined.
\end{quote}
Note what the conclusion does \emph{not} say: nothing global, and nothing
about varying the endpoints, hence nothing about varying the phases.

\paragraph{H6 is not in the theorem; it is in our reduction.} The reduced
Hamiltonian is not conserved, so $\det=0$ can mean $h(t)=0$ rather than a rank
drop. Since $\lambda_{rv}\!\cdot\!f_{rv}=-1+(T/c)\lm$ vanishes only at
$\lm=c/T$, and $\lm$ decreases monotonically to $\lm(\tf)=0$, the condition
$\lm(0)<c/T$ excludes it outright.

\section{What we may therefore claim}
\begin{quote}
\emph{Every hypothesis of the theorem has been checked and holds. If they hold
exactly, the arc is a strict strong local minimiser among trajectories with
the same endpoints and the same departure and arrival phases. The evidence for
two of them --- (H-a) and (H-b) --- is numerical and sampled: positivity is
tested at sampled times, and the conjugate condition is a sign test at
finitely many points, so an even-order zero or two zeros inside one segment
would not be seen.}
\end{quote}
Three words that do \emph{not} belong in that sentence: \emph{global}
(nothing here is), \emph{proved} (two hypotheses are sampled), and
\emph{optimal phases} (the phases are held fixed; a phase optimum would need
its own transversality conditions, which continuation in a phase does not
impose).

\section{What would strengthen it}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Validated (interval) integration of the variational equations, giving a
sign-definite enclosure of the determinant and a rigorous bound behind the
rank margin. Only this turns ``sampled'' into ``proved''.
\item An independent Morse-index count of the discretised second variation ---
a different instrument, not a finer sampling of the same one.
\item Transversality in the phases, if the phases are ever to be optimised
rather than held.
\end{enumerate}
