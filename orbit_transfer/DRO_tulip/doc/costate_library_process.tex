\begin{abstract}
We record the production process behind our first costate library: a catalog
of converged minimum-time low-thrust transfer solutions
$(\lambda_0, t_f)$ over the (departure phase $\times$ arrival phase) torus of
a DRO$\to$tulip transfer in the Earth--Moon CR3BP. The pipeline has three
steps: (1) a \emph{direct} collocation sweep maps the torus and manufactures
full state and costate trajectories; (2) a \emph{multiple-shooting} solver
refines each cell's costates to machine accuracy, seeded by those saved
trajectories; (3) every refined entry is fed to the production single-shooting
solver (\texttt{pumpkyn.cr3bp.tfMin}) and must be accepted unchanged. The
first library holds 126 verified entries; every one passes the acceptance
gate in seconds, and direct and indirect transfer times agree to a median of
$2\times10^{-8}$ time units across the torus.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and why a library}

The indirect (PMP) formulation of the minimum-time low-thrust transfer reduces
to a root-finding problem in the eight unknowns $z = [\lambda_0;\, t_f]$:
seven initial costates and the flight time, driven to satisfy
\begin{equation}
r(t_f) = r_f, \qquad v(t_f) = v_f, \qquad \lambda_m(t_f) = 0, \qquad
H(t_f) = 0 .
\label{eq:bcs}
\end{equation}
Single shooting solves \eqref{eq:bcs} quickly \emph{from a good guess} and
hopelessly otherwise: the guess is the whole game. A \emph{costate library} ---
converged $z$ indexed by problem parameters (here the two orbital phases;
next, thrust and DRO period) --- turns every future solve into a lookup plus a
seconds-long polish.

\section{Step 1: Direct solve (the map-maker)}

A Hermite--Simpson collocation transcription ($N=800$ intervals, Sundman-type
mesh, lifted $t_f$, 500-km lunar altitude floor) is swept over the
$12\times12$ phasing torus by a two-wave strategy: wave~0 attempts a sparse
sublattice from cold seeds; wave~1 floods warm starts from each solved cell to
its torus neighbors. A cell is counted \emph{green} only under continuous
verification: the solved control history is re-integrated end-to-end at high
accuracy and must arrive within map tolerance (100~km, 10~m/s). Solver
success certificates alone never color a cell.

Every completed cell immediately saves its full data product: states,
controls, time grid, $t_f$, the \emph{sign-resolved costate trajectory} at all
nodes (from the Hager covector mapping of the NLP defect multipliers), the
phase pair, endpoint states, and the orbit and thruster definitions. Saving
costate \emph{trajectories}, not just initial values, is what makes Step~2
possible.

\section{Step 2: Refinement with \texttt{ms\_tfmin} (the precision-maker)}

Collocation duals are locally faithful but only ${\sim}10^{-3}$ accurate.
Measured over all 132 green cells, flying the PMP control law from the
collocation-derived $\lambda_0$ misses the target by 12{,}700--558{,}000~km
(median 47{,}500~km): single shooting amplifies seed error by roughly
$10^{3}$ over a 4-ND spiral, so \texttt{tfMin} from such seeds effectively
cold-starts and can grind for hours. The cheap diagnostic --- one PMP flight
per cell --- should always be run \emph{before} spending solver time.

\texttt{ms\_tfmin} therefore solves \eqref{eq:bcs} by \emph{multiple
shooting}: the arc is split into $K$ segments whose junction states are
additional unknowns, which caps the error amplification per segment. All
dynamics, Hamiltonian evaluations, and segment state-transition matrices come
from \texttt{pumpkyn} calls (\texttt{tfMinProp} in its 210-state STM mode;
\texttt{tfMinEoM} for $H$ and $\partial H/\partial y$), so conventions match
\texttt{tfMin} exactly and the converged $z$ is interchangeable with a
\texttt{tfMin} solution. The nonlinear system is solved by trust-region
dogleg with the analytic block Jacobian; a \emph{$K$-ladder} ($12 \to 24 \to
48$) escalates segmentation for rough seeds. Junction seeds come from the
cell's saved state and costate trajectories. Guard rails: per-cell wall
budgets, iterate sanity bounds, and try/catch around segment propagation
(an unguarded wild iterate can collapse the integrator).

First-library outcome: 126 of 132 cells converged to residuals
${\sim}10^{-11}$ with end-to-end PMP flight misses of meters, at 1--25
seconds per cell.

\section{Step 3: Acceptance against \texttt{pumpkyn.cr3bp.tfMin}}

Each refined entry is handed to the production single-shooting solver as its
initial guess. The acceptance criterion is strict: \texttt{tfMin} must return
the seed essentially unchanged ($\lVert \Delta z\rVert < 10^{-6}$) within
seconds --- proof that the entry is a root of the production solver's own
residual, usable downstream with zero adaptation.

First-library outcome: \textbf{126/126 passed}, maximum $\lVert \Delta
z\rVert = 2.8\times10^{-8}$, maximum wall time 2.4~s. As an independent
cross-check of both methods, the direct and indirect transfer times agree to
a median of $2\times10^{-8}$ ND time units over the torus.

\section{Packaging and growth axes}

The shipped artifact is a self-describing MATLAB structure
(\texttt{costate\_lib\_dro\_tulip.mat}): both orbit definitions with
reconstruction recipes (the DRO is selected \emph{by period} --- the $\tau$
of \texttt{getDRO} is the orbit period in ND time), CR3BP constants, thruster
parameters, per-entry phases in fraction/ND/days, $\lambda_0$, $t_f$, the
\texttt{tfMin}-ready \texttt{z8}, verification metrics, and an availability
grid over the torus. Companion files: a nearest-entry picker and a worked
example script.

The pipeline generalizes along three axes: \emph{thrust} (continuation ladder
per cell toward the 1--15~N regime, with multiple-shooting fallback across
revolution-count transitions), \emph{DRO period} (one library sheet per
$\tau$; together the $\Delta V$--period--phasing map), and \emph{orbit pair}
(endpoints from any of pumpkyn's catalogued families).
