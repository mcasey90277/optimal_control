# Phase 2 exit-criteria review (2026-07-28) — BOTH REVIEWERS: NO

Reviewed BEFORE any Phase 2 run, to preserve pre-registration.

Consensus: the criteria as written cannot support their conclusions. Six blocking
issues found by both independently; two more found only by GPT-5.6-terra.

---

## GPT-5.6-terra

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 1 / P2–P4` -- Equal switch counts, paired switch times, and decreasing displacement do **not** establish branch identity. Distinct state/costate paths can share all three, while a correct branch can fail a window-based switch match because a near-zero or grazing switch is classified differently. **Corrected criterion:** interpolate each pair to a common physical-time grid and require (i) scaled primal-path distance, (ii) control/active-set distance away from switching neighborhoods, (iii) switching-function sign pattern, and (iv) scaled IPOPT KKT-multiplier/active-bound pattern to remain continuous under the continuation predictor. Pre-register scales and a separation test against the nearest competing solution. States, controls, constraint multipliers, and bound multipliers are available from a collocation solve; this is evidence, not a proof of global branch identity.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 1 / P1–P4` -- `Solve_Succeeded` plus a nodal defect bound verifies only that IPOPT solved the transcription. A wrong continuous trajectory, or a switch misrepresented between nodes, can pass. **Corrected criterion:** independently reconstruct and propagate the piecewise control/trajectory with a higher-accuracy integrator, report terminal-condition error and integrated dynamics residual, and require these to converge consistently with the transcription sequence.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 1 / SUCCESS` -- Two “valid windows” and monotone shrinking differences do not make a Richardson limit admissible. They can occur in a pre-asymptotic mixture, in a continuation drift, or by coincidence; the 25% agreement rule supplies no uncertainty model. **Corrected criterion:** use at least three branch-confirmed consecutive refinement ratios from a geometrically comparable mesh family; report fitted-model residuals, leave-one-out sensitivity, and a conservative extrapolation interval over plausible models such as \(m_0+a h+b h^2\), not a point Richardson value alone.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 1 / h and pre-registered prediction` -- Node count is not a valid scalar \(h\) when physical steps vary by about \(36\times\), unless every refinement is a nested/homothetic subdivision of the same physical mesh. The stated two-point limits are algebraically sound only conditional on a specified scalar mesh-size sequence and an assumed pure power law. **Corrected criterion:** record each grid’s interval vector and define the refinement variable before fitting, preferably \(h_{\max}\) for nested uniform subdivision or an explicitly justified switch-sensitive mesh metric; require a fixed refinement ratio in that metric. Otherwise label the 1377.84–1377.87 range as a heuristic prior, not a numerical prediction.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 1 / two inherited points` -- Different solve paths do not inherently invalidate two samples, but the present evidence does not establish that they are samples of one discrete continuation branch. Treating their difference as branch discretization can therefore be wrong. **Corrected criterion:** restate it as: “Conditional on subsequent common-grid primal/KKT continuity tests confirming branch identity, these observations suggest a downward change of approximately 0.242 kg.” Do not pre-register a narrow limit interval from them.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 1 / FAILURE` -- Failure of warm-chained IPOPT to retain a branch diagnoses failure of this continuation procedure, not absence of a continuously trackable branch, much less a property of the problem class. A correct branch may be missed by a right physical solution because the basin narrows or the interpolation crosses a discrete active-set boundary. **Corrected criterion:** classify failure as “untracked by the registered continuation method” unless at least one independent branch-following method also fails: e.g. pseudo-arclength continuation with a trajectory-distance constraint, continuation in mesh adaptation strength, or a fixed-structure multiphase/switch-time formulation. Report solver loss, continuation fold, and infeasibility separately.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 1 / warm-chain design` -- Seeding every finer solve from the “best known” solution can intentionally change basin and branch, defeating the stated purpose of holding the production branch. The down-projection retry is useful but cannot distinguish inadequate seeding from branch loss. **Corrected criterion:** start from the named production-branch solution, use a predictor-corrector continuation constraint/tube on the common-grid trajectory, and release that tube only after recording whether the constrained corrector is infeasible, loses KKT regularity, or merely fails unconstrained IPOPT convergence.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 1 / p in [0.8, 2.4]` -- This is a heuristic plausibility screen, not evidence that surviving estimates are discretization orders. It can admit a wrong result near 1 or 2 and reject a real order affected by nonuniform meshes, switch degeneracy, or a mixed expansion. **Corrected criterion:** do not use this interval as an admissibility gate; report it as an H1/H2 comparison band only after model-fit and sensitivity diagnostics pass.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 1 / 25%, bounded extrapolation` -- “Within 25% of their mean” is arbitrary, ill-conditioned for signed/near-zero quantities, and materially determines SUCCESS versus PARTIAL. The bounded-extrapolation rule is also arbitrary and can reject a valid slowly convergent sequence while allowing an unstable fit. **Corrected criterion:** pre-register absolute and relative tolerances derived from solver/integration uncertainty, require stable extrapolation under omission of any one level and under both \(h\)-metrics/models, and report the resulting interval rather than binary admission.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 2` -- \(|p_{\rm finest}-p_{\rm previous}|<0.25\) has no meaning without uncertainty in each effective-order estimate; ratio-of-differences estimates can be extremely noisy when successive changes are small. The “within 0.3” bands are arbitrary and can decide which hypothesis is claimed. **Corrected criterion:** fit competing \(m_0+a h\), \(m_0+b h^2\), and \(m_0+a h+b h^2\) models jointly, with numerical uncertainty propagated; support H1 or H2 only when the corresponding leading coefficient is identifiable and the competing model is demonstrably worse by a pre-registered residual/information criterion.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 2 / 1.3–1.7 rule` -- “Supports NEITHER” is a false trichotomy. A genuine \(a h+b h^2\) expansion can yield a stable effective order in that interval over the available range and is informative about crossover, cancellation, or insufficient refinement. **Corrected criterion:** classify 1.3–1.7 as “mixed/pre-asymptotic expansion consistent with both terms”; report estimated coefficients and crossover scale, and do not claim either pure H1 or pure H2.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 3` -- “Genuine SOLVER reason” is undefined and can be assigned after seeing failures; “a comparison exists” does not ensure scientific comparability. **Corrected criterion:** predefine accepted status codes and resource limits, require identical physical constraints/objective reporting, independently checked feasibility, and a registered comparison metric for every converged ELFO seed. Classify all other outcomes as implementation/infrastructure failures, not scientific nulls.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 4` -- Two finer levels and successful down-projections do not establish that the flagship survived its “strongest available adversary”; they establish survival against a small, path-dependent seed family. Conversely, nonconvergence of the selected finer solves does not establish a resolution ceiling. **Corrected criterion:** state the null narrowly as survival against the registered \(2\times/4\times\) down-projection protocol; distinguish resource cap, solver failure, and demonstrated infeasibility. Require common-grid branch/feasibility checks and a registered best-of-seeds rule before comparing masses.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 5` -- The option/normalization variants are not random draws from a null distribution, so their spread is not a statistical \(\sigma_{\rm row}\). It conflates algorithmic-path sensitivity, transformations that may materially alter the initial point, and genuine basin multiplicity. With \(K\ge5\), “\(5\sigma\)” has neither a calibrated false-positive meaning nor a stable estimate. **Corrected criterion:** report a deterministic nuisance envelope over registered immaterial variants, separately report deliberately varied seed-family/basin outcomes, and require a claimed gain to exceed the maximum verified nuisance envelope plus feasibility/integration uncertainty. Do not call either quantity sigma unless repeated random sampling and a distributional model are registered.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 5 / gain test` -- A mass gain exceeding a spread can still be a wrong comparison if the two solutions differ in feasibility, continuous-time residual, termination convention, or branch. A real but smaller gain may be wrongly discarded solely by the arbitrary multiplier five. **Corrected criterion:** compare paired solves under identical mesh, objective, bounds, and independent continuous validation; report every measured gain with its uncertainty interval, reserving “robust basin gain” for gains whose lower confidence/envelope bound is positive.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Ordering` -- Step 5 should precede any conclusion that a basin gain is real, including the scientific conclusions in Steps 3–4 and the Phase 2 exit. It need not precede calculation of a deterministic mesh sequence, but its numerical/integration uncertainty must enter effective-order and extrapolation diagnostics. **Corrected criterion:** perform per-row reproducibility and validation calibration before declaring any better-basin result; use its uncertainty budget in Steps 1–2 as well.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 6` -- A \(0.5\) increase in estimated \(p\) does not demonstrate second order: \(0.6\to1.1\) passes, while a genuinely second-order aligned scheme can fail because of finite-range mixed terms. “Alignment” is not operationally verified, and FAILURE conflates an unimplemented/incorrect alignment construction with refutation. **Corrected criterion:** require measured switch-to-breakpoint mismatch \(o(h)\), retained breakpoints under every refinement, matched physical branch and comparable degrees of freedom, and an aligned-ladder model whose leading \(O(h)\) coefficient is statistically/numerically indistinguishable from zero while \(O(h^2)\) is identified. Reserve refutation for a validated alignment implementation that fails this comparison.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Phase 2 exit` -- A successful Step 1 as written cannot yield a defensible discretization-error bar; it yields an unquantified, model-dependent extrapolation. One 10 N branch also cannot answer whether error bars are obtainable “for this problem class.” **Corrected criterion:** exit only with a branch-confirmed, independently validated sequence and a reported conservative interval covering mesh metric, fit-model, continuation, and solver/integration sensitivity; scope any negative result to the registered row, branch, solver, and continuation protocol.

**VERDICT: NO.** The single most valuable change is to replace unconstrained warm-chaining with explicit predictor-corrector branch continuation on a common-grid primal/KKT trajectory tube, and to make that continuation test—not switch count/correspondence—the prerequisite for every order and extrapolation claim.

## Gemini 3.1 Pro

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 1 (P2-P4 Branch Identity)` -- Switch count and placement alone do not guarantee two meshes occupy the same topological branch; different local minima can share a bang-bang structure with shifted continuous states. Corrected criterion: Add P5: The L2 norm of the difference between the fine state/costate trajectories and the interpolated coarse state/costate trajectories must shrink monotonically, and the switching function (derived from the costates) must exhibit identically signed slopes at corresponding zero-crossings.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 1 (Pre-registered Prediction)` -- Deriving an asymptotic limit from two points generated via different solve paths conflates mapping discretization error with basin-hopping, rendering the numerical prediction invalid. Corrected criterion: Treat the 1377.84–1377.87 kg window as an a priori hypothesis, not a mathematical baseline. Measure actual $p$ using a minimum of three strictly chained points from a single consistent solver sequence.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Thresholds (25% mean and p bounds)` -- Averaging exponent $p$ across windows is mathematically malformed (error scales non-linearly), and tightly restricting $p \in [0.8, 2.4]$ arbitrarily excludes pre-asymptotic mixed-order regimes. Corrected criterion: Replace the mean variation check with a requirement that the coefficient of determination ($R^2$) for the $m(h) = m_0 + C h^p$ fit across a minimum of three windows is $> 0.95$. 

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 1 (Design for Holding a Branch)` -- Warm-chaining a coarse NLP onto a finer grid does not protect against escaping a shallow minimum as the discretized landscape morphs with $h$. A FAILURE outcome could misattribute the inherent landscape topology to a discretisation method limit. Corrected criterion: Construct a multi-phase, fixed-structure formulation where switch times are explicit NLP variables. Compare the collocation limit specifically against this exact minimum to isolate pure discretization error.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Step 2 (Stop Rule)` -- Declaring $p \in [1.3, 1.7]$ "supports NEITHER" hypothesis is a false trichotomy; a standard mixed truncation error ($E(h) = Ah + Bh^2$) will naturally yield fractional effective orders until $h$ is infinitesimal. Corrected criterion: If local order remains in $[1.3, 1.7]$, explicitly fit the mass sequence to $Ah + Bh^2$ across at least 4 contiguous levels. Only classify as NEITHER if the mixed-model residual fails to shrink.

- **[BLOCKING]** `Step 5 (Reproducibility Scale)` -- Varying solver heuristics (clipping, tolerances) to establish a noise floor conflates algorithmic termination jitter inside a flat basin with true topological basin multiplicity. A 5*sigma bound on solver noise cannot validate a macro-scale topological shift. Corrected criterion: Define `sigma_row` based on the objective landscape flatness at the minimum, strictly bounded by the IPOPT `tol` and constraints. A basin is only distinct if the difference in objective exceeds the strictly defined local optimality gap, independent of solver settings.

- **[IMPORTANT]** `Coverage and Ordering` -- The study evaluates gains and limits before establishing the measurement bounds for those gains. Corrected criterion: Step 5 (Reproducibility Scale) must be executed as Step 0. The noise floor must be measured before assessing branch identity or down-projections.

VERDICT: NO. 
The single change that most improves the phase is to promote Step 5 to Step 0 and redefine `sigma_row` to measure mathematical landscape flatness (optimality gap), rather than solver configuration jitter.
