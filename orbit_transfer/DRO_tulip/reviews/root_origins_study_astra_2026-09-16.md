## Overall assessment

The script contains useful measurements, but several conclusions are stronger than the experiment supports. The most serious issues are:

1. **The direct solves do not request the stipulated all-burn problem.**
2. **The certification seed violates the supplied junction-array contract.**
3. **The basin hunt classifies solutions at different endpoints by their flight-time difference. That is not a basin test.**
4. **Certification failure can still produce “CERTIFIED root” prose.**
5. **The indirect continuation banks junction states but reconstructs its next guesses from a sensitive, unfenced full-arc propagation instead.**

This is a static review against the supplied contracts. Where an omitted implementation might tolerate a contract violation, I distinguish that from a demonstrated runtime failure.

## Quantities and indexing that are right

These are not defects:

## Findings: problem definition and numerical correctness

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:246–247, 304–305, 579–580` -- **Every direct configuration omits `thrLock=true`.** The supplied solver defaults to `thrLock=false`, so it can optimize throttle as well as direction. That is not the stipulated all-burn problem subsequently solved by `ms_tfmin`. `maxUnit` only checks the norm of `alpha`; it does not establish that throttle equals one. **Fix:** set `thrLock=true` for cold, ladder, Sundman handoff, and hunt solves, and independently check node and midpoint throttle against one. Preserve `thrMin` or, preferably, maximum throttle error in the records. Existing results need their actual throttle histories audited before being presented as all-burn results.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:504–509, 529–535` -- **`itCur.Y` has the wrong documented shape for the certification seed.** `ms_tfmin` returns **14×K junction starts**, explicitly excluding the propagated endpoint. `certify_root` requires a seed with **14×(K+1)** columns. Assigning `itCur.Y` directly violates that contract; depending on the engine, it can error or misinterpret the segment count. The “full K+1 junction states” comment is also false for the banked output. **Fix:** append the endpoint obtained by propagating the final short segment, verify `size(seedR.Y,2)==numel(seedR.tGrid)`, and distinguish starts-only solution records from endpoint-inclusive seeds.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:459–470, 504–510` -- **The indirect ladder does not actually warm-start from its banked junction states.** It reflies the entire previous solution from `zCur` and interpolates that flight. This reintroduces exactly the full-arc amplification that multiple shooting and the “identifiability rule” are meant to avoid. Saving junctions does not help if the continuation ignores them. **Fix:** reconstruct the previous trajectory piecewise from `itCur.Y` using short-segment propagation, or resample that segmentwise representation directly. Then stretch normalized time and rebuild mass for the new engine.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:474–479` -- **The flown-miss test is fail-open for NaNs.** If `mk` is NaN, `mk >= num.gateKm` is false, and the candidate can become `best`. The propagation time output is discarded, so a prematurely terminated flight is not explicitly rejected either. **Fix:** require finite `zt`, finite flight samples, positive flight time and mass, and confirmed arrival at `zt(8)`, then test `isfinite(mk) && mk < num.gateKm`.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:474–503` -- **An “accepted” indirect rung is screened only by the solver’s convergence flag and full-flight position miss.** There is no full-flight velocity gate, lunar/Earth clearance check, or explicit use of `tol.R`. Final certification does not prevent an unsafe intermediate rung from being used to construct later guesses. **Fix:** add a common rung-validation result containing residual, position/velocity witness errors, mass validity, and physical clearances. State clearly whether “accepted” means merely “usable numerical continuation seed” or “physically validated root.”

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:259, 333–334, 397, 586` -- **The acceptance predicates ignore important diagnostics that the solver deliberately exposes.** For separated Hermite–Simpson, `maxDefect` does not include `maxInterp`; the output contract explicitly computes interpolation residual separately. `tfSpread`, checking lifted-time continuity, is also ignored. Cold, handoff re-solve, and hunt acceptance omit checks that the direct ladder applies, including terminal and direction errors. **Fix:** use one shared direct-feasibility predicate covering `maxDefect`, `maxInterp`, `tfSpread`, endpoints, direction norm, throttle, mass, and finite values. Report which component failed.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:320, 334, 585–586` -- **`perisKm` is not a certified periselene.** The supplied `altMinKm` implementation checks nodes only—not even the returned Hermite–Simpson midpoint states, much less the continuous trajectory. A node-safe arc can penetrate the floor between nodes. **Fix:** label it `minNodeMoonRadiusKm` until independently validated; check midpoints as a cheap screen and use interval propagation or dense, convergence-checked sampling for physical clearance claims.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:304–305, 395–406` -- **The direct and indirect formulations can also differ through an active clearance constraint.** The direct solution is constrained to stay above a lunar floor; the stated indirect PMP has no state-constraint multiplier or boundary-arc treatment. If the floor binds, its direct covectors are not generally costates of the unconstrained indirect problem. **Fix:** establish that the clearance constraint is inactive along the harvested trajectory, or implement the appropriate state-constrained PMP. A subsequent successful unconstrained shoot can be useful, but must not automatically be called the same constrained direct extremal.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:534–535` -- **Certification does not receive the user’s physical and gate settings.** In particular, the supplied implementation obtains `m0kg` from `opts`, defaulting to 150; putting it in `B.problem` does not set that option. Changing `op.m0kg` can therefore leave reported propellant/final masses wrong. Likewise, `num.clearKm` and `num.gateKm` are not passed as `moonKmMin` and `gateKm`. Defaults happen to agree today. **Fix:** forward these options explicitly and expose the certification tolerances used by this study.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:540–558` -- **Certification failure is misreported as a root identity result.** When `b3` is true and `C.ok` is false, `sameRoot` becomes false and the text says “A DIFFERENT ROOT.” When `b3` is false, the script unconditionally says it demonstrated a “CERTIFIED root.” Neither follows from a failed certificate. **Fix:** branch first on `C.ok`. On failure, print “candidate not certified; family/reference comparison unavailable,” regardless of whether the target engine was reached.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:516–518, 534–535, 574–584` -- **Certification can polish one root, while the hunt and saved `reached` record continue using the pre-polish root.** `C.z` and `C.Y` are neither adopted nor saved in `S`. Thus the object used downstream is not literally the object whose certificate was just reported. **Fix:** retain both the input candidate and complete certification output; when certification succeeds, build downstream seeds from the certified solution using a documented grid reconstruction.

## Findings: basin identification and experiment design

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:565–567, 587–604` -- **The basin hunt compares flight times for different boundary-value problems.** Changing `sA` changes the pinned arrival state. A single smooth family can change flight time by far more than 2% across those phase jumps, and unrelated families can have nearly equal flight times. Consequently, “same basin,” “ANOTHER BASIN,” and the count `nOther` are unsupported classifications. **Fix:** first continue the held family to each target phase, then compare the cold/warm direct result against that same-phase baseline using trajectory/root diagnostics. Without that baseline, print only “candidate at another phase, X% faster/slower than source.”

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:525–526, 541–545` -- **A 1% flight-time match does not identify “THE SAME ROOT.”** Even at identical endpoints and engine settings, distinct roots can have close or equal flight times. Conversely, a difference establishes distinct numerical solutions only after accounting for accuracy; it does not by itself prove different connected families. **Fix:** say “flight time agrees within 1%” and reserve root identity for a comparison of states, controls/costates, or a demonstrated continuation connection.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:176, 275–279` -- **`uniquetol` does not implement the advertised relative basin test.** By default, its tolerance is scaled by the maximum absolute value in the input array, not by each candidate/reference flight time. Adding an unrelated long-duration result can therefore change how two existing results are grouped. More fundamentally, this counts flight-time clusters, not basins. **Fix:** rename the statistic accordingly and define its scaling explicitly—e.g. an absolute ND-time tolerance with `DataScale=1`, or an explicitly documented relative clustering rule. Basin identification needs more than this scalar.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:20–24, 232–244, 278–285` -- **The cold experiment isolates the requested mesh input, but does not isolate “basin selection” from discretization and convergence effects.** Endpoints, engine, time guess, scheme and cold-start policy are held fixed; there is no visible warm-start leakage. That part is sound. However, changing N also changes the finite NLP, its internal discretized guess, conditioning, computational cost and achievable accuracy. Flight-time disagreement alone does not distinguish different physical extrema from those effects. **Fix:** take each converged candidate to a common fine discretization or indirect validation and show that distinct roots survive. Describe the current result as deterministic mesh sensitivity of the cold solve, not a measured random basin-selection mechanism.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:20, 259–265, 273–282` -- **“Every mesh converges” contradicts the supplied September measurement.** N=800 terminated at `Maximum_Iterations_Exceeded`. The implementation is better than the prose: it prints that status and excludes the run from `good`, so it does **not** count the unconverged iterate as a converged basin. **Fix:** make the introduction conditional on the measured results. Retaining an iteration-limited row is sound if it is clearly labelled an iterate, not an extremal; preserve its status in the saved record too.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:281–282` -- **The lottery summary makes two untested universal claims.** `success && maxDefect < tol.defect` does not certify a continuous minimum-time PMP extremal, and “None of them is the campaign’s root” is printed without any reference-agreement test. “Nothing the solver printed says which is which” also confuses an exit status with the returned trajectory and flight time, which plainly contain distinguishing information. **Fix:** say “converged discrete NLP solutions,” test and report reference-time agreement, and explain that solver success does not provide a family label or optimality certificate.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:238–244` -- **The historical table mixes incompatible meanings of “periselene.”** A Moon-centred radius cannot be −343 km. That datum must be an altitude or another signed clearance measure; as altitude it corresponds to radius 1394.4 km and correctly indicates penetration. Current printed tables use radius. **Fix:** recover and state the historical quantity’s convention, then convert the whole historical table consistently. Also qualify the 7.8 m datum as a **worst-interval position error**: it is not automatically a global endpoint error or minimum certificate.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:89–100, 188–189, 260, 540–545` -- **The fixed reference time is not tied to a reference problem definition.** A reader can edit phase, orbit, mass, thrust or Isp while retaining `op.tfDays=17.7976`; the script then still presents it as the campaign’s answer “here.” `b3` only compares the reached engine to the edited target, not to the reference’s original cell and engine. **Fix:** store the reference with immutable problem metadata and disable comparisons unless every relevant parameter matches. Call it the supplied reference unless its later certification is separately documented.

## Findings: ladder mathematics and interpretation

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:321–351` -- **The band arithmetic is right; “BASIN JUMP” is too strong.** It correctly tests a step against `[1/3,3]` times its scaled guess, and exempting the first cold rung is appropriate. But a poor exponent or a sharp turn on the same branch can fail the band, while a jump to a nearby-time branch can pass. Worse, `~inBand` is reported before `~oR.success`, so an unconverged iterate can be labelled a basin jump. **Fix:** first require solver and feasibility success; then report “continuation plausibility-band failure / possible branch change.” The measured 16× step would correctly be refused, but that validates this screen as a useful heuristic, not a universal branch detector.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:123, 299–302, 316, 370–372` -- **The time-scaling heuristic does not establish “the same burn made longer,” nor that delta-V must rise.** At fixed exhaust speed, mass consumption depends on `Tnd*tf`. If the stated \(t_f\propto T^{-0.6}\) scaling held exactly, `Tnd*tf` would decrease as thrust decreased, so ideal delta-V would decrease—not necessarily rise slowly. **Fix:** present the exponent as a predictor, print the measured changes without a universal causal claim, and use trajectory/branch evidence before saying it is the same burn.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:461–481` -- **The exponent sweep changes guesses coherently, but selection is not branch-preserving continuation.** For thrust changes, it really changes `tfGuess`, the junction time grid and the rebuilt all-burn mass row. Keeping other state/costate samples as an imperfect guess is legitimate. However, selecting the shortest returned root among all exponents can deliberately switch branches; no indirect time-band, trajectory-distance or winding-continuity criterion is applied. **Fix:** retain the candidate set and distinguish “continue the held branch” from “search several basins and take the shortest candidate.”

- **[EFFICIENCY]** `root_origins_study.m:461–481` -- **An unsuccessful Isp-only step is repeated with the same seed for every exponent.** When `TN==Tcur`, the exponent has no effect. The loop only breaks after an accepted candidate, so failures repeat the identical deterministic solve up to five times. **Fix:** use a single exponent/attempt for Isp-only changes, unless the retry deliberately changes another solver or seed parameter.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:466–467` -- **The depletion guard’s explanation is wrong.** All-burn mass is `m(tf)=1-Tnd*tf/cnd`; actual exhaustion occurs at `tf=cnd/Tnd`. The implemented `0.6*cnd/Tnd` limit retains at least **40% mass**, rather than preventing only exhaustion. It can exclude valid high-consumption roots. **Fix:** name and expose this as a seed propellant-fraction policy, record when it skips an exponent, and do not treat exhaustion of this restricted search as evidence that no physical root exists. Validate final mass separately.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:500–501` -- **`revs` is not a winding/revolution count in the usual sense.** It is the total variation of projected Moon-centred azimuth in the rotating XY plane, divided by \(2\pi\). Reversals accumulate positively; an open transfer has no intrinsic integer winding number; projected azimuth is ill-conditioned near the lunar polar axis; and insufficient sampling can defeat `unwrap`. **Fix:** name it `totalRotatingAzimuthTurns`, also report signed net azimuth change, specify rotating versus inertial interpretation, and verify angular sampling resolution before using it diagnostically.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:57–62, 134–152, 436–442, 488–494, 558` -- **The observed refusals do not establish an intrinsic “winding wall, not a sensitivity one,” or a wall that is “not of the code.”** The 0.81→1.31 diagnostic shows increasing angular excursion. It does not distinguish a fold, ill-conditioning, poor seeds, the depletion cutoff, insufficient budget, or infrastructure failure. The statement that “only a different exponent” reaches the right basin is likewise not established. **Fix:** call this a stall of the configured continuation/search policy. To diagnose a physical branch limit, add smaller/arclength steps, sensitivity/rank diagnostics, budget studies and a complete failure record.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:137–141` -- **The Isp explanation has a correct local mechanism but overstates its consequence.** At fixed thrust and elapsed burn time, lower Isp depletes mass faster and increases subsequent `T/m` while mass remains positive. It also reduces available burn duration and changes the entire boundary-value solution. It is not generally guaranteed to help continuation. **Fix:** retain the acceleration explanation, but label the earlier Isp stage as an empirically useful route for the cited experiment rather than a general remedy.

## Findings: Sundman handoff

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:395–403` -- **Using a separate Sundman re-solve is legitimate, but the script does not establish that it retained the ladder’s branch.** The acceptance test checks only solver success and one defect metric. There is no comparison with `oPrev.tf`, no trajectory comparison on common physical times, and no complete feasibility screen. The comparison printed later is Sundman direct versus shot—not original plain-time direct versus Sundman. **Fix:** print and save both transitions, compare aligned trajectories and flight times, and run the same feasibility checks before selecting `oH8`.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:390–393, 411–415` -- **The multiplier diagnostics are displayed but not enforced, and their evidential reach is overstated.** `dgB.lamTOK` is available but unused; no sign-vote threshold is applied. A constant `lambda_t=1` supports sign/scale consistency but cannot verify station association: shifting a constant row between nodes and midpoints leaves it unchanged. **Fix:** give mapping diagnostics their own explicit checks and report `lamTOK` and notes. Describe midpoint association as a rule implemented by `duals_to_costates`, not something proved by the constant time-costate row. The measured 100% vote, `lambda_t=1`, and one-iteration residual \(4.59\times10^{-12}\) are excellent handoff evidence, but not proof of every mapping claim or of branch identity.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:397–406` -- **The fallback hides why the Sundman solve failed and does not validate harvest inputs.** The direct solver can return empty `lamDef` after its internal multiplier-extraction catch. `harvest_ms_seed` immediately indexes its first seven rows, so solver success alone does not guarantee harvestability. **Fix:** validate multiplier dimensions, midpoint controls and time nodes before harvesting; record Sundman failure/status explicitly. Failing with a named “dual extraction unavailable” diagnostic is more informative than an indexing error.

## Findings: runtime bounds, records and interface handling

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:405–406, 459–460, 475, 499, 574` -- **Several expensive integrations remain unfenced even when a pool exists.** The initial handoff is solved before pool creation, and all listed full-arc propagations run on the client outside `fenced`. Any can crawl near a singularity or abort the script. A solver’s between-iteration `wallSec` check cannot bound one stuck integration. **Fix:** create the pool before the handoff and fence every potentially crawling operation, preferably replacing full-arc reconstruction with short-segment reconstruction. Retain a clearly named poolless policy.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:462, 471–475` -- **`attemptSec` is not a per-rung wall-clock cap.** A call can start just before 600 seconds and receive another 330 seconds, plus propagation and cancellation overhead. IPOPT’s `maxCpuSec` is also a CPU-time limit, not a hard elapsed-time fence. **Fix:** pass the remaining rung budget into both solver and fence caps, account for validation work, and distinguish CPU, soft solver-wall, and hard external-wall budgets in the printed configuration.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:156–159, 474–494, 588–589` -- **Solver failure is repeatedly interpreted as information about solution existence.** A timed-out hunt does not show “there is no nearby basin,” and IPOPT failure is not “no solution.” On the indirect ladder, worker errors, timeouts, nonconvergence, depletion skips and flown-miss rejection collapse into “REFUSED.” Two such results then become “THE WALL.” **Fix:** return and save a failure taxonomy, including infrastructure errors. Print “no converged candidate within this attempt/budget,” not “no solution” or a diagnosed physical wall.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:574–578` -- **The hunt bypasses the duplicate-time protection used elsewhere.** The supplied `flight_to_junctions` explicitly removes duplicate propagator time samples because they can occur; these raw spline interpolations do not. They are outside the per-probe `try`, so a duplicate can abort the whole hunt. **Fix:** reuse the validated resampling helper or deduplicate once before interpolation. Also check primer norms rather than silently converting a zero primer into a zero direction through the `eps` denominator.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:676` -- **The wrapper promises row or column endpoints but passes columns to an API documented as row-only.** `phase_state` supplies columns, and `rv0(1:6)` preserves that orientation. The shown terminal diagnostic normalizes `rvf`, but the omitted solver body cannot be assumed to normalize every use. **Fix:** canonicalize at this boundary with `rv0(:).'` and `rvf(:).'`. This is a direct-API contract issue, not the propagator-orientation bug mentioned in the question.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:420–423, 516–518, 534–535, 621–631` -- **The MAT record omits the certificate and final verdict.** `C` and `gateStatus` are never assigned to `S`, so “run saved” does not mean the advertised certification evidence was saved. **Fix:** save the full certificate, gate statuses, configuration/tolerances, code/solver provenance, and both pre- and post-polish roots.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:631, 689–693` -- **Saving can fail silently while the script prints that it succeeded.** This can also leave an older file at the advertised path. **Fix:** make `saveq` return success, issue at least a warning with the exception on failure, and print the last successful checkpoint status rather than an unconditional assertion.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:55–56, 346–361, 483–497, 596–604` -- **“Saves after each rung” does not include refused rungs, and failed probes can disappear from the denominator.** Direct and indirect failures are not appended to their records. Hunt exceptions do not append an `H` entry, so “of N probes” means returned records, not attempted probes. **Fix:** append one attempt record for every rung/probe, including status, failure reason, guess, budget and elapsed time. Print attempted, converged and accepted counts separately.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:355–358, 591, 596` -- **The direct ladder and hunt do not bank enough information to reproduce or anchor their discoveries.** `S.direct` contains scalar summaries only; `S.hunt` loses `X`, `U`, midpoint data and multipliers. “Anchor it” is not actionable for earlier probes once `oH` is overwritten. **Fix:** bank each accepted numeric direct solution or a harvested seed, not just its flight time and radius. `returnModel=true` is unnecessary when the wrapper immediately strips the model and the solver returns duals independently.

## Findings: printed gates and remaining prose

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:169–177, 213, 333–339, 405–406, 471–473, 534–535` -- **“One source, used by every printed line AND the verdict” is false.** The orbit seam threshold is hardcoded at `1e-6`; handoff solve tolerance is hardcoded at `1e-11`; indirect acceptance uses `ms_tfmin`’s default tolerance rather than `tol.R`; certification uses numerous unexposed defaults. Direct terminal and direction gates are enforced but their measured values are not printed or saved. **Fix:** separate solve tolerances from acceptance tolerances explicitly, expose them in configuration, and retain a named result for each acceptance predicate.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `root_origins_study.m:366–368` -- **“Direct ladder finished” is tested using thrust alone.** If the user includes multiple Isp stages at the final thrust, an earlier accepted stage can satisfy `b2` even though the requested final row was refused. Also, this print is labelled `B1`, while the final table calls the same condition `B1b`. **Fix:** determine completion by accepted rung index, or compare the complete final engine row, and use one gate label.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:16–46, 621–626` -- **The self-check does not assess all three advertised mechanisms.** It contains ladder, handoff and certification gates, but none for the cold experiment’s conclusion or the basin hunt’s conclusion. Those mechanisms may be skipped, inconclusive, or incorrectly classified while all listed gates pass. **Fix:** give A and the hunt explicit experimental outcomes—preferably `SUPPORTED / NOT OBSERVED / INCONCLUSIVE`, rather than treating unsuccessful searches as machinery failures.

- **[PEDAGOGY]** `root_origins_study.m:203–204, 295–302, 428–434, 489–494` -- **Several explanatory claims are not measurements made by this script.** The orbit getter obtains catalog-based orbit seeds and refines/propagates them, so “never loaded” is inaccurate, although no reference *transfer* is loaded. “Near-impulsive,” “sub-revolution,” “every rung starts next to its answer,” the reason direct shooting supposedly loses the thread, and easier descent from a fast cell are not established by the computations shown. **Fix:** distinguish catalog-orbit provenance from transfer-root provenance; label historical empirical observations as such; and replace universal claims with measured timing, trajectory-distance, angular-excursion and conditioning diagnostics.

## Ranked top-5

1. **Set and verify `thrLock=true` everywhere direct.** Otherwise the direct/indirect bridge is between different control problems.
2. **Repair the starts-only versus endpoint-inclusive seed contract at certification.**
3. **Replace the hunt’s cross-phase flight-time “basin test.”** Compare solutions at identical endpoints against a continued family baseline.
4. **Honor `C.ok`, save the actual certificate, and use the certified solution downstream.**
5. **Continue from banked short-segment trajectories and fence all validation propagation.** The current full-arc reconstruction defeats the main numerical protection of multiple shooting.
