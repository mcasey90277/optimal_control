\begin{abstract}
This document records, with mathematical rigor and physical intuition, how
the DRO$\to$tulip costate libraries were built. We define the dynamical
model (the circular restricted three-body problem in the rotating synodic
frame), the two orbit families and their phasing torus, and the minimum-time
optimal control problem with mass dynamics. We derive the necessary
conditions of Pontryagin's Minimum Principle, whose two-point boundary-value
problem in eight unknowns $z = [\bm\lambda_0; t_f]$ is what a costate
library stores solutions of. We then explain \emph{why} a library is the
right artifact --- the exponential sensitivity of single shooting and the
multi-family structure of the solution landscape --- and describe the
three-stage production pipeline: direct collocation with continuous
verification, covector-mapped multiple-shooting refinement, and acceptance
by the production single-shooting solver. Every design choice cited here is
tied to a measurement made during the campaign.
\end{abstract}

\section{The dynamical model}

\subsection{The circular restricted three-body problem}

Two primaries --- Earth (mass $m_1$) and Moon (mass $m_2$) --- move on
circular orbits about their barycenter; a spacecraft of negligible mass
moves in their combined gravity field. The \emph{circular restricted
three-body problem} (CR3BP) studies the spacecraft's motion. Its single
dimensionless parameter is the mass ratio
\begin{equation}
\mu \;=\; \frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2} \;=\; 0.012150585609624 .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Nondimensionalization.} Lengths are scaled by the Earth--Moon
distance $l^\ast = 389{,}703.264829278$~km, time by
$t^\ast = 382{,}981.289129055$~s (chosen so the primaries' angular rate is
unity), so one nondimensional (ND) time unit is $4.4327$~days and one
revolution of the Moon is $2\pi$ ND $= 27.85$~days. Velocities scale by
$l^\ast/t^\ast \approx 1.0175$~km/s.

\subsection{The rotating (synodic) frame}

All work is done in the frame that rotates with the primaries: the $x$-axis
points from the barycenter through the Moon, $z$ along the orbital angular
momentum, $y$ completing the right-handed triad. Earth sits at $(-\mu,0,0)$
and the Moon at $(1-\mu,0,0)$, both \emph{fixed}. The price of a fixed
geometry is fictitious accelerations; the equations of motion are
\begin{equation}
\ddot{\Rv} \;=\; \bm g(\Rv)
\;-\; 2\,\bm\omega\times\dot{\Rv}
\;-\; \bm\omega\times(\bm\omega\times\Rv),
\qquad \bm\omega = \hat{\bm z},
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
with the two-body gravities
\begin{equation}
\bm g(\Rv) = -\frac{(1-\mu)(\Rv - \Rv_E)}{\lVert \Rv-\Rv_E\rVert^{3}}
             -\frac{\mu\,(\Rv - \Rv_M)}{\lVert \Rv-\Rv_M\rVert^{3}} .
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with the effective potential
$U(\Rv)=\tfrac12(x^2+y^2)+\frac{1-\mu}{r_1}+\frac{\mu}{r_2}$,
equation~\eqref{eq:eom} reads $\ddot x - 2\dot y = U_x$,
$\ddot y + 2\dot x = U_y$, $\ddot z = U_z$.

\paragraph{Intuition.} The centrifugal term tilts the landscape outward;
gravity wells sit at the two primaries; their competition creates the five
Lagrange equilibria. The Coriolis term does no work but curls every
trajectory --- it is why low-energy motion in this frame looks like
spirals and loops rather than conic arcs, and why families of
\emph{periodic} orbits (DROs, halos, tulips) exist at all: in the rotating
frame the time-dependence of the gravity field disappears, so closed orbits
become possible and the Jacobi integral
$C = 2U - \lVert\dot{\Rv}\rVert^2$ is conserved (ballistically; thrust
changes it).

\subsection{The two orbit families}

Both terminal orbits come from the pumpkyn/pumpkynPie catalogs: a seed
state from a lookup table, sharpened to machine periodicity by differential
correction (\texttt{cont\_np}), then propagated one period.

\paragraph{DRO (departure).} Distant retrograde orbits are stable, planar,
retrograde loops around the Moon. The family is \emph{parameterized by its
period}: $\texttt{getDRO}(\tau)$ returns the member whose period is $\tau$
ND. The libraries use $\tau = 1.0$ (period $4.43$~d, lunar distances
$27$--$30$~Mm); the catalog sweeps $\tau \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 3\}$ within the
measured admissible range $[0.05, 3.25]$.

\paragraph{Tulip (arrival).} Tulip orbits are $z$-excursing resonant orbits
whose ground track over the lunar south pole draws $N_p$ petals. The
resonance locks the period to $(N_p-2)/(N_p-1)$ lunar revolutions,
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\rm tulip} = \frac{N_p-2}{N_p-1}\,2\pi ,
\end{equation}
e.g.\ $N_p=7 \Rightarrow \tau = 5\pi/3 = 23.21$~d. The two branches
$pm=\pm1$ are exact mirror images under $z\to-z$ --- a symmetry of
\eqref{eq:eom} --- so every solution for one branch maps to the other by
flipping $z$-components of states \emph{and costates}; only one branch is
ever computed.

\subsection{Phasing and the torus}

A transfer's endpoints are points \emph{on} these orbits, labeled by phase:
the fraction $f\in[0,1)$ of the period elapsed past the catalog reference
state. The state at phase $f$ is obtained by propagating one period and
interpolating. Because both phases wrap, the space of (departure, arrival)
choices is a \emph{torus}; the library grids it (12$\times$12 for the
single-pair libraries, 6$\times$6 per catalog sheet). Every point of the
torus is a complete optimal control problem.

\section{The optimal control problem}

\subsection{Model with mass dynamics}

The state is $\bm x = [\Rv; \Vv; m] \in \mathbb{R}^7$ with $m$ normalized
by the initial mass. Control is a thrust direction $\uh$
($\lVert\uh\rVert=1$) and throttle $\delta\in[0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\Rv} = \Vv, \qquad
\dot{\Vv} = \bm a_{\rm 3B}(\Rv,\Vv) + \delta\,\frac{T_{\max}}{m}\,\uh, \qquad
\dot m = -\,\delta\,\frac{T_{\max}}{c},
\label{eq:dyn}
\end{equation}
where $\bm a_{\rm 3B}$ collects gravity, centrifugal and Coriolis terms of
\eqref{eq:eom}, $T_{\max}$ is the ND thrust acceleration scale
($T_{\max} = \frac{T[\mathrm N]}{m_0}\frac{(t^\ast)^2}{1000\,l^\ast}$) and
$c = I_{sp}\,g_0$ the ND exhaust velocity. Given endpoints
$\Rv(0),\Vv(0)$ and $\Rv(t_f),\Vv(t_f)$ fixed by the chosen phases, and
$m(0)=1$ with $m(t_f)$ free, the \textbf{minimum-time problem} is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\;\uh(\cdot),\,\delta(\cdot),\,t_f}\; J = \int_0^{t_f}\! 1\, dt .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pontryagin's Minimum Principle}

Adjoin costates $\bm\lambda = [\lr;\lv;\lambda_m]$ and form the
Hamiltonian (with the normal multiplier absorbed as $+1$):
\begin{equation}
H \;=\; 1 + \lr^{\!\top}\Vv
      + \lv^{\!\top}\!\Big(\bm a_{\rm 3B} + \delta\frac{T_{\max}}{m}\uh\Big)
      - \lambda_m\,\delta\,\frac{T_{\max}}{c}.
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
Minimizing $H$ pointwise gives the control law. The direction enters only
through $\lv^{\!\top}\uh$, so
\begin{equation}
\uh^\star = -\,\frac{\lv}{\lVert\lv\rVert}
\qquad\text{(the \emph{primer vector} law)},
\end{equation}
and the throttle through the \emph{switching function}
\begin{equation}
S = -\,\frac{\lVert\lv\rVert\,c}{m} - \lambda_m,
\qquad
\delta^\star = \begin{cases} 1, & S<0\\ 0, & S>0 . \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For minimum time the engine runs continuously in practice
($\delta\equiv1$; confirmed numerically on every library entry, which is
also why $\Delta V = c\,\ln\!\frac{1}{m(t_f)}$ with
$m(t_f) = 1 - T_{\max}t_f/c$ is \emph{exact} for these solutions, and why
at fixed thrust minimum time and minimum $\Delta V$ are the same transfer).

The costates obey $\dot{\bm\lambda} = -\partial H/\partial \bm x$:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\lr} = -\Big(\frac{\partial \bm a_{\rm 3B}}{\partial \Rv}\Big)^{\!\top}\!\lv,
\qquad
\dot{\lv} = -\lr
            -\Big(\frac{\partial \bm a_{\rm 3B}}{\partial \Vv}\Big)^{\!\top}\!\lv,
\qquad
\dot\lambda_m = \delta\,\frac{T_{\max}}{m^2}\,\lVert\lv\rVert .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Intuition.} $\lv$ (the primer) points opposite the optimal
thrust; its magnitude measures how much a unit of velocity change is worth
right now. $\lr$ is the shadow price of position; the Coriolis coupling
rotates the pair continuously, which is why costates in the rotating frame
oscillate on roughly the orbital timescale.

\subsection{The boundary-value problem}

Free final mass gives $\lambda_m(t_f)=0$; free final time gives the
transversality condition $H(t_f) = 0$ (with the $+1$ convention of
\eqref{eq:H}). Collecting, a candidate optimal transfer is a root of the
\emph{shooting residual}
\begin{equation}
\bm R(z) =
\begin{bmatrix}
\Rv(t_f;z) - \Rv_f\\[2pt]
\Vv(t_f;z) - \Vv_f\\[2pt]
\lambda_m(t_f;z)\\[2pt]
H(t_f;z)
\end{bmatrix} = \bm 0,
\qquad
z = \begin{bmatrix}\bm\lambda(0)\\ t_f\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^8,
\label{eq:bvp}
\end{equation}
where the trajectory is generated by integrating states and costates from
$t=0$ under the PMP control law. Equation~\eqref{eq:bvp} is exactly the
system \texttt{pumpkyn.cr3bp.tfMin} solves; \textbf{a costate library is a
catalog of its roots}, indexed by the problem parameters (phases, thrust,
orbit-family parameters).

\section{Why a library: sensitivity and solution families}

\subsection{Exponential sensitivity of single shooting}

The state--costate flow of \eqref{eq:bvp} is Hamiltonian and, near these
orbits, strongly unstable: linearized errors grow with the state-transition
matrix, whose norm grows roughly exponentially along multi-revolution
arcs. Measured on the $\tau=1$, $N_p=7$ torus: costate initial conditions
accurate to $\sim10^{-3}$ (the best a direct transcription's multipliers
deliver, see \S\ref{sec:covector}) produce terminal misses of
12{,}700--558{,}000~km over 4--7 revolution transfers --- an amplification
of order $10^3$. Single shooting from such a seed is effectively a cold
start; from a library root it converges in one step. \emph{The guess is the
entire difficulty; the library is the guess.}

\subsection{Multiple families}

The solution landscape is not single-valued. At one phase pair and thrust
we measured two converged, feasible minimum-time transfers with
$t_f = 0.40$ vs $0.60$~days; across the phasing torus, neighboring cells'
$t_f$ jumps by up to 75\% across \emph{family walls} (median smooth
variation is 5--8\% per grid step). Which family a solver lands on depends
on its seed. Two practical consequences: maps must be built by
\emph{continuation} (each solution seeding its neighbor) to stay on one
family per region, and independent cold solves produce patchwork
(``blocky'') maps even when every individual solve converged.

\section{The production pipeline}

Three stages; an entry joins a library only after passing \emph{all
three gates}.

\subsection{Stage 1: direct collocation, continuously verified}

The OCP is transcribed by Hermite--Simpson collocation: states and controls
at $N$+1 nodes, dynamics enforced by separated Simpson defect constraints
on each interval, $t_f$ a decision variable, throttle and unit-direction
bounds, and a lunar-altitude path constraint ($\geq 500$~km) --- without
which the optimizer happily flies through the Moon. The NLP is solved by
IPOPT via CasADi.

A converged NLP is \emph{not} accepted on the solver's word. The
\textbf{continuous verification gate} re-integrates the solved control
history end-to-end with a high-accuracy integrator: the flown trajectory
must arrive within tolerance (100~km, 10~m/s). This matters because defect
satisfaction is not accuracy: a coarse mesh can satisfy its own discrete
equations along a ``trajectory'' no spacecraft could fly (measured: NLP
defect $10^{-14}$ coexisting with $O(1)$ true error on an early
mesh).

\subsection{Stage 1.5: the covector mapping}
\label{sec:covector}

\paragraph{Principle.} Write the transcribed NLP with dynamics enforced by
defect constraints $\bm c_k(\bm X, \bm U, t_f) = \bm 0$, one vector per
interval, and let $\bm\mu_k$ be their KKT multipliers. Stationarity of the
NLP Lagrangian with respect to the state variables,
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\bm x_k} J \;+\; \sum_j \bm\mu_j^{\top} \nabla_{\bm x_k}\bm c_j
\;=\; \bm 0,
\end{equation}
is, term by term, a \emph{discretization of the continuous adjoint
equation} $\dot{\bm\lambda} = -\partial H/\partial \bm x$. This is the
covector mapping principle (Hager's analysis for Runge--Kutta classes; the
weight-scaled version for pseudospectral methods): \textbf{the multipliers
are samples of the costate}. The library pipeline exploits it to obtain
costate trajectories from a solver that was never told costates exist.

\paragraph{The map is transcription-specific.} \emph{Which} samples, with
what scaling, depends on the scheme. For trapezoidal collocation the
multipliers associate naturally with the \emph{nodes}. For
Hermite--Simpson --- our transcription --- the discrete adjoint places
$\bm\mu_k$ at the interval \emph{midpoint} $t_k + h_k/2$ (adopting the node
association instead injects a half-step phase error into every seed; found
in external review, since fixed), and the mapped values show mild order
reduction near the arc endpoints. For Legendre--Gauss--Lobatto
pseudospectral methods the multipliers must additionally be scaled by the
inverse quadrature weights. A reusable implementation therefore takes the
scheme as part of its contract.

\paragraph{Conventions decide sign and scale.} The multiplier of
$\bm c = \bm 0$ is defined only up to the conventions of the NLP: how the
defect is normalized (an $h$-scaled defect scales $\bm\mu$ by $1/h$), the
constraint's sign, minimization sense, and the solver interface's own
choices --- notably, CasADi's \texttt{opti.dual} returns duals in a
\emph{canonicalized orientation} and must be bypassed in favor of
\texttt{opti.lam\_g} indexed by constraint rows (a lesson that once cost a
10--60$^\circ$ primer misalignment). Residual global sign is fixed
empirically: the mapped primer $-\lv/\lVert\lv\rVert$ must align with the
solved thrust direction, decided by majority vote over the first ten
stations. With a lifted final time the multiplier paired with the $t_f$
degree of freedom supplies a free consistency check: for our conventions it
must equal $+1$ exactly (it is $-H$, and $H(t_f)=0$ with the $+1$
normalization); junk solutions announce themselves by violating it by
orders of magnitude. Under a Sundman time transformation the mapping
acquires a clock-rate factor which vanishes here because $H \equiv 0$ along
optimal arcs of the transformed problem.

\paragraph{Limits, stated plainly.} (i)~On arcs where a path constraint is
\emph{active} (the altitude floor), the inequality multipliers inject
additional terms: the mapped $\bm\lambda$ is the costate of the
\emph{constrained} problem and is not a valid seed for the unconstrained
BVP \eqref{eq:bvp} --- refinement re-converges the unconstrained problem
and absorbs this where the floor is inactive at the solution. (ii)~The
accuracy ceiling is $\sim10^{-3}$ regardless of care: mapped costates are
\textbf{seeds for multiple shooting, never shooting-quality solutions}
(\S3.1's 12{,}700--558{,}000~km measurement is the proof).

In practice two details carry the stage: the sign vote above, and saving
the full costate \emph{trajectory}, not just $\bm\lambda(0)$ --- stage~2's
junction seeds exist only because of it.

\subsection{Stage 2: multiple-shooting refinement (\texttt{ms\_tfmin})}
\label{sec:mstfmin}

Because $10^{-3}$-accurate costates cannot survive single shooting
(\S3.1), refinement solves the BVP \eqref{eq:bvp} by \emph{multiple
shooting}. This subsection gives the complete formulation implemented in
\texttt{ms\_tfmin}.

\paragraph{Unknowns and segment maps.} Let
$\bm y = [\Rv;\Vv;m;\lr;\lv;\lambda_m] \in \mathbb{R}^{14}$ be the
augmented state, whose flow $\dot{\bm y} = \bm f(\bm y)$ combines the
dynamics \eqref{eq:dyn} and the costate equations under the PMP feedback
law ($\uh = -\lv/\lVert\lv\rVert$, $\delta$ from the switching function).
Fix \emph{normalized breakpoints} $0=\sigma_1<\dots<\sigma_{K+1}=1$ with
$\Delta\sigma_k = \sigma_{k+1}-\sigma_k$, so segment $k$ spans physical
time $\Delta\sigma_k\,t_f$. Define the segment map
$\bm\Phi_k(\bm y;t_f)$: the value at the segment's end of the flow started
at $\bm y$ and integrated for $\Delta\sigma_k t_f$. The unknown vector is
\begin{equation}
\bm p \;=\; \big[\underbrace{\bm\lambda_1}_{7};\;
\underbrace{\bm y_2;\dots;\bm y_K}_{14(K-1)};\;
\underbrace{t_f}_{1}\big] \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{14K-6},
\end{equation}
where segment 1's state part is pinned to the known departure
$[\Rv_0;\Vv_0;1]$ so only its costates $\bm\lambda_1$ are free.

\paragraph{Residual.} With
$\hat{\bm y}_{k+1} := \bm\Phi_k(\bm y_k; t_f)$ (and
$\bm y_1 = [\Rv_0;\Vv_0;1;\bm\lambda_1]$),
\begin{equation}
\bm R(\bm p) \;=\;
\begin{bmatrix}
\hat{\bm y}_2 - \bm y_2\\
\vdots\\
\hat{\bm y}_K - \bm y_K\\[3pt]
\hat{\Rv}_{K+1} - \Rv_f\\
\hat{\Vv}_{K+1} - \Vv_f\\
\hat\lambda_{m,K+1}\\
H(\hat{\bm y}_{K+1})
\end{bmatrix}
\in \mathbb{R}^{14(K-1)+8} = \mathbb{R}^{14K-6},
\end{equation}
a square system. At a root, the concatenated segments form one continuous
extremal satisfying every condition of \eqref{eq:bvp}; setting $K=1$
recovers single shooting exactly, which is why a converged $\bm p$ is
interchangeable with a \texttt{tfMin} solution.

\paragraph{Why it works.} Linearized error through one segment grows by at
most $\lVert\Phi_k\rVert \sim e^{L\,\Delta\sigma_k t_f}$ rather than
$e^{L t_f}$: the $\sim10^{3}$ amplification that destroys single shooting
is replaced by $K$ factors of order $10^{3/K}$, each absorbed by its own
junction unknown. Seed quality requirements drop correspondingly --- which
is exactly what covector-mapped seeds can meet, since the saved direct
trajectory supplies \emph{every} junction, not only the initial point.

\paragraph{Analytic Jacobian.} Let
$\bm A_k = \partial\bm\Phi_k/\partial\bm y\in\mathbb{R}^{14\times14}$ be
the segment state-transition matrix (obtained from pumpkyn's augmented
propagation of $\dot{\bm A} = (\partial\bm f/\partial\bm y)\bm A$), and
$\bm f_{k+1} := \bm f(\hat{\bm y}_{k+1})$. Because the breakpoints are
fixed in $\sigma$, the only $t_f$-dependence of segment $k$ is its
duration, so by the chain rule
$\partial\hat{\bm y}_{k+1}/\partial t_f = \bm f_{k+1}\,\Delta\sigma_k$ ---
and crucially, junction states are \emph{independent} unknowns, so no
dependence accumulates across segments. The Jacobian is block
bidiagonal-bordered:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \bm R}{\partial \bm p} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\bm A_1 \bm S & -\bm I & & & \bm f_2\Delta\sigma_1\\
 & \bm A_2 & -\bm I & & \bm f_3\Delta\sigma_2\\
 & & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots\\
 & & & \bm T\,[\text{cols of } \bm y_K] & \bm G
\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $\bm S \in \mathbb{R}^{14\times7}$ selects the costate columns of
segment 1 (its states being pinned), and the terminal rows are
\begin{equation}
\bm T = \begin{bmatrix} [\bm A_K]_{1:6,\,:}\\ [\bm A_K]_{14,\,:}\\
\big(\partial H/\partial\bm y\big)\bm A_K \end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb{R}^{8\times14},
\qquad
\bm G = \begin{bmatrix} [\bm f_{K+1}]_{1:6}\\ [\bm f_{K+1}]_{14}\\
\big(\partial H/\partial\bm y\big)\bm f_{K+1}\end{bmatrix}\Delta\sigma_K .
\end{equation}
Every ingredient ($\bm A_k$, $\bm f$, $\partial H/\partial\bm y$) comes
from pumpkyn's own routines, so conventions match \texttt{tfMin} exactly
--- the same functions, called verbatim. (An external review verified this
assembly independently.)

\paragraph{Solve strategy and guards.} The square system is solved by
trust-region dogleg (the same globalization \texttt{tfMin} uses; a plain
damped-Newton loop stalled on 2 of 3 pilot cells). Rough seeds escalate a
\emph{segment ladder} $K = 12 \to 24 \to 48$; each rung costs seconds
because the analytic Jacobian makes an iteration $\approx$ one full-arc
STM propagation. Guard rails, each purchased by an incident: iterate
sanity bounds whose rejection residual exceeds \emph{any reachable true
residual} (a smaller constant was once accepted as an ``improvement''),
try/catch around segment propagation (a wild iterate once collapsed the
integrator and took the process with it), and wall budgets.

Refined entries reach residuals $\sim10^{-11}$ and their $\bm\lambda(0)$,
flown open-loop under the PMP law, arrives within meters.

\subsection{Stage 3: acceptance by the production solver}

Finally each refined $z$ is handed to \texttt{pumpkyn.cr3bp.tfMin} as a
seed. \textbf{Pass = the solver returns it unchanged}
($\lVert\Delta z\rVert < 10^{-6}$; observed $\leq 10^{-9}$) in seconds.
This gate makes physics consistency structural rather than asserted: every
library entry is a root of the production solver's own residual, usable
downstream with zero adaptation. As an independent cross-check, direct and
indirect flight times agree to a median of $2\times10^{-8}$ ND across the
torus --- a certification of \emph{both} methods at every phase pair.

\section{The thrust axis: ladders and continuation}

Thrust enters as a family parameter. At high thrust (15~N on 150~kg,
$0.1$~m/s$^2$) transfers are sub-revolution and nearly impulsive: cold
direct solves converge in seconds. At low thrust, transfers wind many
revolutions and cold solves are expensive and unreliable. The catalog is
therefore built by a \textbf{downward thrust ladder}: anchor each phase
pair at 15~N, then walk down the rung set
$\{15,12,10,7,5,3,2,1.5,1\}$~N with each rung warm-started from the one
above (states from the propagated solution, control from the PMP law,
$t_f$ scaled by an empirical $t_f \sim T^{-p}$ with $p$ steepening from
$\sim0.56$ to $\gtrsim1.7$ down the band; the seed's mass history rescales
by both the time stretch and the thrust ratio, since $\dot m \propto T$).
Continuation keeps a phase pair on \emph{one} solution family; independent
solves do not (\S3.2).

The ladder's floor is physical: coverage collapses where transfers cross
one departure-orbit revolution (measured: 63/144 pairs at 1~N, 20/144 at
0.5~N for the $\tau=1$ DRO). Below the boundary lives the genuinely
low-thrust regime, which the $0.07$~N library reaches with different
machinery (Sundman-regularized meshes, cold flood seeding over the torus)
--- the two sheets are neighbors, not points on one continuum.

\section{The catalog and its admissible box}

Orbit-family parameters (DRO period $\tau$; tulip $N_p$) become catalog
axes. ``Reasonable'' members are decided by measurement, not intuition:
propagate each candidate one period and require periselene altitude
$\geq 500$~km and maximum lunar distance $\leq 100$~Mm (lunar vicinity,
including L1/L2). For the DRO family this admits $\tau\in[0.05,3.25]$; all
tulip petal counts 3--14 pass. A periodicity guard excludes family members
whose differential correction did not truly converge (their metrics are
garbage), with the check written NaN-safely as $\lnot(\text{err}<\text{tol})$.

The resulting coarse catalog: 16 sheets ($\tau\in\{0.5,1,2,3\}\times
N_p\in\{5,7,9,12\}$), $6\times6$ phasing, 9 rungs --- 3{,}936 three-gate
entries at 89--97\% phase-pair coverage per sheet, built in $\sim$26
unattended hours ($\sim$25~s per verified solution). Headline physics:
at fixed 5~N the \emph{orbit-pair} choice spans $\Delta V$
1.18--4.09~km/s ($3.5\times$), dominating the phasing effect
($\lesssim2\times$ within a sheet, and shrinking with decreasing thrust as
slow spirals average the geometry).

\section{What the pipeline verifies --- and what it does not}
\label{sec:checks}

The pipeline's checks deserve honest classification, because they prove
different things. Three tiers, in ascending strength:

\paragraph{Tier 1: gross-error tripwires.} Feasibility screens that prove
nothing about optimality but keep junk out of the machinery: NLP
convergence with defect $<10^{-9}$; pre-flight node screens (altitude
floor, plausible $t_f$) before any unbounded integration; the magnitude of
$\lambda_t$ (junk solutions violate the $+1$ identity by orders of
magnitude); periodicity and NaN guards on family members; mass-fraction
validity on catalog entries.

\paragraph{Tier 2: consistency checks.} The solution agreeing with itself
or with \emph{our} statement of the equations. Defect satisfaction is
internal consistency of the discretization --- with the canonical
counterexample on record (defect $10^{-14}$ coexisting with $O(1)$ true
error). The \textbf{flown-control gate} is the strongest member of this
tier and merits its precise label: \emph{true independent verification of
dynamical feasibility} --- a separate high-accuracy integrator confirms
the trajectory is physically flyable; a feasible but wildly suboptimal
trajectory passes identically. The multiple-shooting residual
($\sim10^{-11}$) verifies our statement of the PMP conditions; the
open-loop PMP flight to meter-level arrival confirms our junctions agree
with a single-arc integration. Structural identities --- continuous burn,
$\Delta V = c\ln(1/m_f)$ against propagation, $H(t_f)=0$ via
$\lambda_t = +1$ --- are consistencies with the theory, not independent
facts.

\paragraph{Tier 3: extremality verification with real independence.} The
\texttt{tfMin} acceptance gate hands each entry to an \emph{independently
implemented} residual --- different author, different code for dynamics,
Hamiltonian and STMs --- and requires the entry back unchanged
($\lVert\Delta z\rVert\leq10^{-9}$ observed). This catches convention and
implementation errors that no amount of internal checking can. The
direct--indirect cross-agreement (median
$|t_f^{\rm dir}-t_f^{\rm ind}| = 2\times10^{-8}$ over 126 phase pairs) is
two methodologically alien solvers converging to the same object.

\paragraph{What none of it verifies.} Every library entry is a
\emph{verified extremal}: a solution of Pontryagin's \emph{necessary}
conditions, independently confirmed. Three things remain unproven:
(i)~\textbf{local optimality} --- no second-order (Jacobi) condition is
tested, and over 4--7-revolution arcs an extremal can pass a conjugate
point and cease to be even a local minimum without any tier-1--3 check
noticing; (ii)~\textbf{global optimality} --- our own measurements refute
any such claim (multiple extremal families at one phase pair, 50\% apart
in $t_f$); ``minimum time'' in a library means \emph{best extremal on the
family the continuation stayed on}; (iii)~normal extremals are assumed
(the $+1$ normalization); abnormal extremals are invisible to the
formulation.

\paragraph{A sobering meta-observation.} The two subtlest real defects
found in this program --- the midpoint-vs-node station association
(\S\ref{sec:covector}) and a missing thrust-ratio factor in continuation
mass scaling --- passed \emph{every} runtime gate, because the pipeline is
self-healing: refinement re-converges from degraded seeds. Both were found
by external code review. Gates verify solutions; review verifies code;
neither substitutes for the other.

\subsection{Closing the local-optimality gap: the conjugate-point test}
\label{sec:conjugate}

The one missing verification with a practical route is second order. Along
an extremal, consider neighboring extremals launched from the same initial
state with perturbed initial costates: with the augmented STM
$\bm\Phi(t,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{14\times14}$ partitioned into state/costate
blocks, a perturbation $\delta\bm\lambda(0)$ (with
$\delta\bm x(0)=\bm 0$) produces
\begin{equation}
\delta\bm x(t) \;=\; \bm\Phi_{x\lambda}(t,0)\,\delta\bm\lambda(0).
\end{equation}
A time $t_c\in(0,t_f)$ where $\bm\Phi_{x\lambda}$ (restricted to the
position--velocity rows and $\lr,\lv$ columns, the $6\times6$ block
relevant to our fixed-endpoint conditions) becomes \emph{singular} is a
\textbf{conjugate point}: a nontrivial neighboring extremal returns to the
same state, and the second variation ceases to be positive definite ---
the extremal is no longer locally minimizing beyond $t_c$. The practical
test is therefore to monitor
\begin{equation}
J(t) \;=\; \det\!\big[\bm\Phi_{x\lambda}(t,0)\big]_{6\times6}
\end{equation}
for zero crossings on $(0, t_f)$. The implementation is nearly free in our
architecture: the multiple-shooting Jacobian (\S\ref{sec:mstfmin}) already
propagates every segment's $14\times14$ STM, so $\bm\Phi(t,0)$ accumulates
as the running product of segment matrices, and $J$ is evaluated at
segment boundaries as bookkeeping on quantities already computed. We
implement this as a \emph{necessary-condition indicator} attached to every
refined solution (crossing detected $\Rightarrow$ flagged not locally
optimal; no crossing $\Rightarrow$ strong evidence of local optimality),
with the standard refinements for the free final time and the free-mass
channel documented as a follow-on. This upgrades the library's claim from
``verified extremal'' toward ``verified locally minimizing extremal'' at
the cost of a determinant per segment.

\section{Conclusion}

The library concept converts the indirect method's greatest liability ---
seed sensitivity --- into a data product: solve each region of parameter
space once, with a direct method that needs no costate guess; distill each
solution to machine-accurate costates by multiple shooting; certify against
the production solver; and index by the parameters missions care about.
The pipeline is family-agnostic (endpoints enter only through a shared
orbit provider) and is now being applied to the L2-southern halo family.
