Delivering the Constellation: Minimum-Fuel Low-Thrust Transfers from GTO to Lunar Tulip Orbits

Tulip-shaped cislunar orbits offer superior navigation geometry over the Moon's
southern hemisphere, but a constellation is only as practical as the
trajectories that populate it. This presentation addresses the delivery
problem: moving a rideshare smallsat from geostationary transfer orbit to a
tulip orbit on continuous low thrust, for the least propellant.

We model a 15 kg spacecraft with 25 mN of electric propulsion (Isp 2100 s) in
the Earth-Moon circular restricted three-body problem. The transfer spirals out
of Earth's gravity well over roughly forty revolutions. We solve it by direct
collocation, with three ingredients that make the problem tractable. A Sundman
regularization changes the independent variable so that the mesh follows the
spacecraft through perigee, where the eccentric spiral is otherwise severely
ill-conditioned. A continuation from a smooth energy-optimal problem to the
true minimum-fuel problem then finds the bang-bang thrust structure on its own,
with no assumption about how many burns there are or where they fall. Finally, a
Pontryagin-steered refinement uses costates recovered from the transcription to
place mesh points where the switches actually are.

At 1.15 times the minimum transfer time -- about 32 days -- the certified
solution needs 3.35 km/s and 2.25 kg of propellant, fifteen percent of wet mass,
across roughly two dozen burn-coast arcs. Dynamics residuals sit at the 1e-14
level, and the solution passes an independent check against Pontryagin's
first-order necessary conditions. We map the full delta-V versus transfer-time
front, and retarget the same pipeline to an elliptical lunar frozen orbit to
compare delivery costs between constellation architectures.

One methodological result deserves emphasis, because it affects how such
transfers should be reported. These solutions are not unique. At a single
transfer time we find at least six distinct local optima, differing in both
propellant and in the number of burns. A systematic search over starting
guesses improves the front by up to 53 grams at short transfer times, while
leaving it unchanged for longer ones -- the solution landscape fragments as the
transfer time approaches its minimum, and settles once there is time to spare.
A single converged trajectory, however tight its residuals, is therefore an
upper bound on propellant rather than the answer; we report the best found under
a documented and reproducible search.

These results show that southern-hemisphere PNT constellations are deliverable
with small rideshare spacecraft, and the method carries over directly to
cislunar transfer design, constellation deployment, and replenishment planning.
