On a nonlinear problem, like the action of a pendulum, something subtle happens. With a big nudge, the neighbors cross near the conjugate point, but not exactly on it. Watch what happens as I shrink the nudge. The crossings march in, closer and closer, to t equals three point three zero one. The error halves every time the nudge halves. So a conjugate point really lives in the limit. It's where infinitely close neighbors refocus. And the rescaled differences? They squeeze onto the Jacobi field, just as before.
