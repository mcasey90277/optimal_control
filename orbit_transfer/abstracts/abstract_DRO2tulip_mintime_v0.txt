Lunar PNT: Minimum-Time Low-Thrust Transfers from a Distant Retrograde Orbit
to Lunar Tulip Orbits

Tulip-shaped cislunar orbits offer superior navigation geometry over the Moon's
southern hemisphere. This presentation addresses the deployment problem: moving
a navigation/relay satellite from a distant retrograde orbit, a natural staging
point in cislunar space, into its tulip orbit on continuous low thrust, as
quickly as possible.

We model a 150 kg spacecraft with 70 mN of electric propulsion (Isp 900 s) in
the Earth-Moon circular restricted three-body problem. The transfer is solved
by indirect optimal control: Pontryagin's principle reduces the problem to
finding the initial costates and the transfer time that satisfy the boundary
conditions, and these eight unknowns are found by shooting.

The minimum-time transfer takes about 17.8 days and requires 0.75 km/s of
delta-V and 12.2 kg of propellant, eight percent of wet mass, at an average
thrust acceleration of 0.05 cm/s^2. Applying the same transfer to a staggered
departure schedule deploys an evenly phased constellation, with every satellite
flying an identical trajectory and arriving at a common formation time.

These results show that a southern-hemisphere PNT constellation can be deployed
from a single cislunar staging orbit using low thrust, in under three weeks per
satellite.
