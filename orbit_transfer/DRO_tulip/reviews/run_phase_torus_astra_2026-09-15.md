The supplied helpers are not sufficient to verify the gate stack, dual conventions, or every mod-1 grid assertion. The findings below distinguish defects visible in this code from limitations of the approved discovery policy.

## Orchestration and recovery

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:95-112` -- **Resume does not establish campaign identity.** Existing state is combined with the *current* phase lists, engine, orbits and tag without checking compatibility. Separately, arcs live in a shared `results` directory and are reused solely by filename (`88`, `124`); two campaigns using the default tag can consume each other’s arcs. This can mix different boundary conditions or propulsion models in one purportedly certified library. **Fix:** persist and validate a campaign manifest containing the physical configuration, grids, anchor root IDs and schema version. Store arcs under the campaign directory and consume an explicit manifest of compatible products rather than a shared glob.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:124-136,322-345` -- **Arc-file existence is neither successful completion nor a safe publication protocol.** The final MAT file becomes visible while `save -v7.3` is still writing it. MATLAB startup failures produce no arc verdict, so the driver waits the entire deadline. A killed driver records no PID/job ownership; restarting before its children finish can launch duplicate writers. On timeout, those children are not stopped. The broad arc pattern also matches `.partial.mat` filenames unless the unseen sheet builder explicitly excludes them. **Fix:** give every arc attempt an ID, record its process/job identity, write an atomic success/failure verdict, and publish the final MAT through a temporary file plus rename. Resume or attach to active jobs; cancel their process groups on abandonment. Keep partial checkpoints out of the sheet’s input manifest and actually use them for continuation recovery.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:164-175,349-359` -- **The revised verdict check rejects old timestamps, not old attempts.** A supervisor from an earlier attempt can finish *after* `before` and satisfy this wait. A touched or partially written verdict is also read immediately rather than awaited until a terminal record. Nothing here establishes exclusive ownership before rebuilding the sheet or synchronously packaging in the same directory. The `packaged` bypass fixes the acceptance log’s stale-verdict failure, but not this race. **Fix:** use a campaign lock and attempt-specific verdicts containing a generation ID and output-manifest hash. Adopt an existing supervisor or stop it before restarting; accept only an atomic terminal record for the expected generation, and establish that the finalizer has exited before modifying its inputs.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:279-285,203` -- **Discovery is not transactionally checkpointed.** A root is saved to `direct_certified.mat` before its anchor exists, and the updated anchor table is not saved until the round ends. If the driver dies in between, the next sheet can select that direct root, but resumed state lacks its anchor. It may no longer be “slow,” so discovery never promotes it and its family never gets arcs. Ordinary `save` also leaves the state vulnerable to interruption during overwrite. **Fix:** commit a journaled root/anchor transaction using immutable root IDs and atomic writes; checkpoint each accepted discovery. On resume, reconcile certified spine roots against the anchor registry and promote eligible unanchored roots before rebuilding the sheet.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:277-285` -- **Anchor names collide whenever discovery improves the same column again.** Both discoveries become `d03`, for example. The second overwrites the first anchor file and appends another row with the same name, while `124` sees the old `d03` arcs and skips walking the new root. This directly defeats discovery of successive families at one phase. **Fix:** give each distinct root an immutable ID, such as column plus root hash or a monotonic discovery number. Never overwrite an existing anchor; bind each arc to that root ID and verify the binding before reuse.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:152-156,166-168` -- **“Spine unchanged” is inferred only from flight time, and existing round-local ribs are never invalidated.** Different roots/families can have equal flight times near a crossing. A finite-to-missing spine transition is also marked unchanged. On a resumed round, even a detected change leaves an existing destination rib untouched; such a file must not satisfy the requirement to walk the new spine. **Fix:** compare root identity, boundary phases and normalized shooting data, not just `TF`. Treat loss of a certified spine as a regression. Record each rib’s source-root and grid/configuration IDs; invalidate mismatched current-generation publications while retaining compatible old roots as *packaging candidates*.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:181-196,362-369` -- **Faster spine roots found by holes/improve do not enter the discovery/continuation loop.** Repackaging includes `fh.file`, but discovery still uses the pre-fill `S`; earlier direct-cell files are later offered only as ribs, not as sheet seeds. Thus a newly certified, faster root at `sD(1)` can improve one catalog cell without ever becoming a spine or anchor. This misses the runbook’s “unattached root faster than its column’s family” trigger. **Fix:** extract certified direct-cell roots at the spine departure phase, append them to the common certified-seed registry, promote faster unattached roots, and refresh the sheet/family map before discovery and termination tests.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:185-188` -- **The repackage stage has no success barrier and still requests ribs.** Unlike the initial packaging call, its returned state is merely logged. If it plans or blocks work rather than packaging, discovery and final publication proceed with an older catalog. Requesting `'ribs', true` also fails to express the specified package/audit/sweep-only operation. **Fix:** use an explicit no-walking packaging mode, require successful package/audit/sweep completion, and verify that the receipt and sidecar belong to the resulting catalog generation before proceeding.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:205-209` -- **The second stopping condition is not the approved fixed-point rule.** It stops with changed spines whenever all spine columns are finite, no anchor was added and the filler certified nothing. Moreover, “nothing certified” can mean timed-out probes or no eligible seeds, not an exhausted search; the first condition can stop despite new off-spine points. **Fix:** remove the extra OR-condition and distinguish convergence, budget exhaustion and stalled/incomplete search. Track pending promotions and untried target/seed combinations; if new catalog roots can enable further work, process that work before claiming a fixed point. Do not retry identical failed probes indefinitely—use a bounded attempt ledger.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:191-229` -- **Discovery in the last allowed round is omitted from the library of record.** Packaging precedes discovery. If round `maxRounds` accepts a faster root, state and the seed file contain it, but `final` receives the pre-discovery catalog and sheet. The result is nevertheless called `done`. **Fix:** before final publication, consume all committed certified roots in a final sheet/package/audit/sweep pass, even if no further arcs are permitted. Report pending continuation and a budget-limited status separately from convergence.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:216-222` -- **Final publication can mix generations.** Missing files are silently skipped while an existing `final` directory is retained; an old receipt, sidecar or picture can therefore accompany a new catalog. **Fix:** build a fresh temporary final directory, require the mandatory products, verify their generation/catalog hashes, and atomically replace the previous final directory. List genuinely unavailable optional products explicitly rather than leaving stale copies.

- **[EFFICIENCY]** `run_phase_torus.m:115,203-209` -- **A successfully converged campaign is not remembered as converged.** If it stops at round 2 with `maxRounds = 6`, the next invocation starts round 3. Plan mode likewise prints another round rather than the specified “nothing to do”; if the loop is already exhausted, plan mode falls through into final copying instead. **Fix:** persist a terminal status, stopping reason and manifest hash. Check that status before planning or launching, and reopen the campaign only for changed inputs, new roots or an explicit override. The finite round bound prevents an infinite round loop within one normal invocation, but not these unnecessary restarts.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:102,316-332` -- **Valid caller paths can make every spawned arc fail.** Anchor paths are checked relative to the caller but used after the child changes directory. The dependency startup path is hard-coded, and the MATLAB executable and redirected output path are not shell-quoted. The background shell’s zero exit status does not establish successful MATLAB startup. **Fix:** canonicalize anchor paths before persisting state, make dependency initialization configurable, correctly escape both shell and MATLAB literals, and obtain startup/exit status through the job protocol. Bound concurrent arc jobs as well: two MATLAB processes per anchor, each starting a pool, can exhaust memory or licenses before any useful continuation begins.

## Discovery soundness

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:248-268` -- **The approved discovery heuristic can systematically miss faster families.** A uniformly slow sheet, or a broad slow plateau whose adjacent increments are below `slowDays`, triggers no probes. Even at a triggered column, the candidate pool contains only neighbouring *winning spines*: another family present among nonwinning certified candidates is invisible. With one family occupying all nearby spines, discovery refuses to search despite being intended to discover another family. **Fix:** search certified roots by family, including nonwinning sheet candidates, anchors and direct roots. Add periodic coverage probes and a widening/global fallback when the local other-family pool is empty. Treat absence of a discovery as heuristic evidence, not proof that the library contains the minimum-time family.

- **[EFFICIENCY]** `run_phase_torus.m:263-268` -- **Wrapped candidate indices are not deduplicated before the three-seed cap.** On the 3×3 acceptance case, offsets `+1/-2` and `-1/+2` repeat the same columns; this explains the duplicated 194-day failures and successful probes in the log. Repetitions can consume every allowed slot and exclude another seed. **Fix:** apply `unique(...,'stable')` to eligible indices, exclude the target itself, and deduplicate by root ID before ranking/capping. Prefer diversity across families rather than several nearly identical seeds.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:249,263-268` -- **Three column steps cease to mean “nearby” on a nonuniform phase list.** Adjacent columns may be separated by most of an orbit, while many tightly packed columns can exclude a geometrically close seed just beyond the index window. Ranking solely by time can then choose the worst arrival-geometry warm start—the expensive failure mode described in the runbook. **Fix:** rank or gate using circular phase distance, `abs(mod(sA-sA_target+0.5,1)-0.5)`, with configurable radius and widening fallback. Keep index adjacency only if that is explicitly the intended policy, not as a substitute for phase proximity.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:274-280` -- **The acceptance rule discards real improvements and useful certified branches.** `1e-3 d` is **86.4 seconds**, not a numerical root-deduplication tolerance. It rejects improvements that the approved “faster than the spine” rule would accept. Only the fastest accepted probe is archived; other certified results are lost, contrary to the runbook’s append-then-decide-whether-to-anchor sequence. **Fix:** archive every distinct certified root with provenance. Separate root deduplication from anchor promotion, use a configurable/error-justified time tolerance, and retain distinct branches that may become competitive elsewhere.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `direct_cell_solve.m:9-11`; `run_phase_torus.m:274-285` -- **A local direct solve followed by PMP certification does not establish a new minimizing family.** It can return the same family, another local minimum, or—if shooting polishing changes branch—a stationary root that is not minimizing. Being faster than the current spine does not resolve this. The current acceptance logic tests neither family novelty nor second-order eligibility itself. **Fix:** distinguish “new certified root,” “new continuation family” and “minimum-eligible catalog entry.” Retain exploratory roots if useful, but require the gate stack’s actual second-order/clearance status for minimum eligibility and preserve competing roots until that decision is made. Multistart and coverage probes improve discovery; they still do not provide a global minimum certificate.

## Phase lists and direct-solve numerics

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `rib_targets.m:29` -- **A fixed `1e-9` exclusion silently removes distinct valid departure phases.** The interface permits arbitrary strictly increasing spacing. For example, a requested phase `5e-10` from the spine is dropped as though it were the spine, including across the mod-1 seam. The signed, unwrapped offset construction otherwise has the right sense. **Fix:** exclude the designated spine entry by identity, or use a matching tolerance strictly smaller than half the minimum circular list spacing. If the numerical machinery cannot resolve such spacing, reject it explicitly and use the same resolution contract in all sheet/rib matchers.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:313,320-327` -- **The fixed ±1 level copies do not cover every permitted unwrapped arc.** With an anchor at `0.97` and the default upward span `1.15`, the arc reaches `2.12`; a requested phase `0.05` has a crossing at `2.05` that is not in `levels`. The analogous problem occurs below `-1`, and arbitrary larger spans worsen it. This is a limitation of the spec’s ±1 prescription as well as the implementation; overlapping turns can contain different roots. **Fix:** generate all integer translates intersecting the actual unwrapped continuation range, and use the same rule in sheet reconstruction. Handle fold excursions by generating levels as intervals are traversed or from the completed arc’s range.

- **[CORRECTNESS]** `direct_cell_solve.m:71-81` -- **The clearance-floor classification is neither an activity test nor a continuous periselene test.** A perfectly unconstrained extremal passing 2 km above the floor is rejected by the arbitrary 5 km margin. Conversely, node samples can miss contact between nodes, including Hermite–Simpson midpoints. Proximity alone also does not establish a nonzero path-constraint multiplier. **Fix:** inspect appropriately scaled clearance multipliers and evaluate clearance on a dense continuous reconstruction or independent integration with periselene event/root finding. Reject genuinely active constrained solutions; allow inactive near-floor candidates into exact-phase certification, then recheck the certified flight’s continuous clearance.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `direct_cell_solve.m:61-81` -- **The direct-cell operation has neither an outer wall deadline nor a failure boundary.** The CPU cap applies only to the collocation solver; seed propagation, solver setup, harvesting and exceptions are outside it. The certification wall limit starts later. One pathological propagation or thrown solver/harvester error can therefore hang or abort the entire multi-day driver instead of rejecting one seed. **Fix:** execute the complete cell attempt in a cancellable worker/process with an outer wall deadline, validate returned arrays and finite values, and convert recoverable exceptions into a structured failed-attempt record. Checkpoint that record so resume does not blindly repeat it.

- **[ROBUSTNESS]** `run_phase_torus.m:78,81-83,166-168` -- **Documented configuration is not honoured.** `.orbits` and `.engine` are dereferenced rather than given their specified campaign defaults. More consequentially, `.rib.wallSec` is never read or forwarded, so a user’s rib budget has no effect. **Fix:** apply the documented physical defaults, parse and validate rib options, forward the wall budget into queue jobs, and persist effective settings in the campaign manifest. Make the supervisor deadline configurable or derive it from queued work and worker count.

The cross-cell warm start is **not inherently wrong because its initial trajectory misses the new endpoints**; an NLP initial guess may be infeasible. Likewise, harvesting duals only as a seed and then certifying at the exact requested phases is the right separation of roles. What cannot be verified here is whether `casadi_mintime_dro` reparameterizes the physical-time samples for its Sundman mesh, and whether `harvest_ms_seed` correctly handles defect scaling, costate sign/normalization and free-time conditions. Those need a direct→harvest→certify regression test with mesh refinement, not an assumed defect based solely on these calls.

## Ranked top-5

1. **Make artifact publication and process ownership generation-safe** — arc-file races and old supervisors can invalidate whole stages or waste their full deadlines.
2. **Validate campaign identity on resume and isolate arc storage** — prevents silently mixing different physical problems and grids.
3. **Make discovery commits atomic and anchor IDs immutable** — otherwise new families can be orphaned or reuse another root’s arcs.
4. **Close the direct-root feedback/publication loop** — promote faster filler spines and include last-round discoveries in `final`.
5. **Broaden discovery beyond slow neighbouring winning spines** — the current policy can systematically leave faster families undiscovered.
