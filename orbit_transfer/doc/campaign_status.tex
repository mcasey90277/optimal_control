\begin{abstract}
Four low-thrust transfer campaigns now exist side by side: elliptic$\to$GEO in
the two-body problem, the same transfer with lunar gravity, GTO$\to$tulip, and
GTO$\to$ELFO. All four are solved by direct collocation; three carry certified
thrust ladders; one carries an externally reviewed physics result. This
document states what is firm, what has been withdrawn, what remains open in
each, and---the question that prompted it---whether to bank the direct work and
begin indirect methods, or whether something closer to hand is worth more.

The recommendation is neither. The highest-value next step is a
\emph{fixed-structure multi-phase formulation with switch times as explicit
decision variables}, because it is not a detour from indirect methods but the
bridge to them: it resolves the branch-identity failure that invalidated two
separate measurement attempts, it supplies the switch-aligned mesh whose order
we have been trying to measure indirectly, and switch times as variables
\emph{is} the indirect structure, solved by a well-conditioned NLP rather than
by shooting. Separately and immediately, a basin sweep is cheap and improves
published numbers, because every certified row examined is beaten on its own
mesh.
\end{abstract}

\section{Campaign 1: elliptic$\to$GEO, two-body}

\subsection{What is firm}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A certified min-fuel thrust ladder from $10$~N to $0.1$~N},
  nine rows including two PSR-refined, every one machine-tight
  ($\sim10^{-14}$ defect).
  \item \textbf{Reproduction of the reference paper's Table 3}: final masses
  $\sim\!1377$~kg against the published $1370$--$1375$, with minimum transfer
  time matching exactly. The deep rungs ($0.2$, $0.1$~N) required a documented
  recipe---\camp{maxIter} is decisive, and \camp{scaleNLP} is actively harmful
  because it fights IPOPT's own scaling.
  \item \textbf{First-order optimality: $9/9$ rows pass}, with primer
  misalignment $0.000^\circ$, sign law $100\%$, KKT stationarity between
  $1.5\times10^{-14}$ and $6.9\times10^{-10}$.
  \item \textbf{The \camp{opti.dual} bug and its fix.} Duals read through
  CasADi's canonicalized orientation were sign-corrupted; reading
  \camp{opti.lam\_g} by constraint row range fixed a $32.4^\circ$ primer
  anomaly to $0.000^\circ$ across two campaigns.
  \item \textbf{This is the campaign where the new machinery works.} The PMP
  residual checker, the basin probe and the redistribution test all run here.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is open}

The $0.5$~N minimum-time wall; the $\sim\!20^\circ$ dual anomaly at the
$10$--$60^\circ$ range (partially explained by the \camp{opti.dual} fix); and
the finding below, which is new and unwelcome.

\subsection{The finding that changes how these numbers should be read}

Re-solving a certified row \emph{on its own unmodified mesh} from a different
starting guess finds a better solution in every case examined:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
row & certified $\mf$ & best found & gain \\
\midrule
$10$~N  & $1377.101$~kg & $1378.117$~kg & $+1.015$~kg \\
$2.5$~N & $1369.791$~kg & $1375.762$~kg & $+5.971$~kg \\
$1$~N   & $1370.357$~kg & $1371.811$~kg & $+1.454$~kg \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Each winner also has lower $\dV$ and, in two of three cases, fewer switches. At
$10$~N a \emph{one-node shift of the throttle profile} is enough to reach a
better basin. ``Certified'' currently means converged, feasible and
first-order optimal---not best found.

\section{Campaign 2: elliptic$\to$GEO with lunar gravity (CR3BP)}

\subsection{What is firm}

This is the campaign with a publishable result.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A full certified ladder $10\to0.1$~N} with lunar third-body
  perturbation, driven by one front door.
  \item \textbf{The Moon's effect mapped against thrust}: roughly $50$~g flat
  at high thrust falling to a $\sim\!30$~g secular floor.
  \item \textbf{The Moon's effect mapped against lunar phase}, which is the
  interesting part: a $\pi$-periodic tidal quadrupole whose \emph{sign flips},
  measured at $+52$, $-56$, $+69$, $-53$~g across four phases. Whether the Moon
  helps or hurts depends on where it is.
  \item \textbf{An externally reviewed technical note} (two rounds, GPT-5.6 and
  Gemini) judged publishable, with figures and a four-panel phase movie.
  \item \textbf{A hard-won solver lesson}: the MUMPS segfault on deep rungs is
  cured by \camp{liftDL} (turning an arrowhead KKT into a banded one) together
  with $\camp{maxIter}\ge4000$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is open}

The deep-rung $\phi_0$ sweep (only $10$~N has the full phase map); stronger
optimality certificates; and the indirect phase, which has not begun.

\section{Campaign 3: GTO$\to$tulip}

\subsection{What is firm}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The flagship min-fuel solution is certified}: a sharp
  many-switch full-spiral transfer, $25$ switches, $2.264$~kg propellant,
  $\dV = 3.370$~km/s, defect $2.4\times10^{-14}$. It required a Sundman
  regularization plus an energy$\to$fuel homotopy plus a no-resample seed map.
  \item \textbf{A thrust ladder from $25$ to $20$~mN}, which unblocked a
  problem recorded for months as a ``fixed-$\tau_f$ topology wall.'' It was not
  a wall; it was continuation step size, and both free-time and step-size
  changes were needed.
  \item \textbf{$\dV$--$t_f$ fronts mapped} for this and the ELFO target.
  \item \textbf{The direct campaign was flattened} into one front door with
  \camp{lib/certify/viz/tests}, replacing two folders that read as rival
  pipelines.
  \item \textbf{The flagship survives a basin probe}---the only row examined
  that does. Six seeds all return $\mf = 0.84906583$ with $25$ switches to
  $10^{-10}$. \emph{Caveat}: the seed family here excludes the down-projection
  seeds that beat every earth row, so this shows local robustness, not
  optimality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is open, and one item is a basin problem}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The $1.01$--$1.11\times$ near-minimum-time band} remains
  unsolved, for both the bang-bang and the smooth energy problem---a
  conditioning wall, not a bang-bang artifact.
  \item \textbf{Two certified optima at $t_f = 1.150\times$, $1.43\%$ apart in
  $\dV$.} Two routes reach $25$ switches, both machine-tight, at the same
  $t_f$, with $\mf$ of $0.847086$ and $0.849066$. This is the basin problem,
  already recorded in this campaign's own TODO before the earth work found the
  same thing systematically.
  \item \textbf{The indirect campaign is stalled}: multiple shooting reaches
  $1.12\times$ with ten certified switches, but the indirect finishing solve
  stalls at $\|R\|\sim0.023$ on terminal-cluster conditioning.
\end{itemize}

\section{Campaign 4: GTO$\to$ELFO}

\subsection{What is firm}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The min-fuel $\dV$--time front is mapped}, eleven $t_f$ rows at
  $\varepsilon = 0$.
  \item \textbf{Minimum time is anchored}: $t_{f,\min} = 6.0962$ ND
  $= 27.02$~days, machine-tight, by a hard all-burn route.
  \item \textbf{The solver is a genuine fork, not a retarget}: the lunar
  capture leg needs a two-primary Sundman clock
  $\kappa = (r_1^{-q} + (r_2/D)^{-q})^{-p/q}$ that a single-primary
  Earth clock cannot resolve, plus free $t_f$ and a ninth slack state.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is open}

The indirect route has not started. The campaign carries a path dependency on
the tulip folder to reach exactly \emph{one} function, and its solver shares
$122$ identical lines ($76\%$) with the tulip's---incidental duplication worth
extracting, but the solvers are genuinely different and should not be merged.
The new PMP and basin machinery has \textbf{not} been run here: it needs its
own adapter for the nine-state two-primary formulation, and a first attempt
failed because it called the wrong solver.

\section{Cross-cutting: the verification layers}

\subsection{What is firm}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The first-order optimality gate} runs on all four campaigns:
  AD-assembled KKT stationarity, primer alignment, transversality on a mapped
  terminal covector, dual sign law, singular-arc and switching-regularity
  checks.
  \item \textbf{The mapped terminal covector retracted a false finding.} The
  earth $2.5$~N row's ``genuine first-order miss'' of $1.265\times10^{-3}$
  resolved to $2.27\times10^{-18}$ once the covector was mapped properly; the
  original reading was a representation artifact.
  \item \textbf{A continuous PMP residual checker}, validated against an
  analytic extremal to $10^{-16}$ and demonstrated sensitive to a deliberately
  misplaced switch at thirteen orders of separation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the residual checker established}

On the earth rows, the per-interval residual is the local truncation error of
the trapezoidal rule: $R_x/h^3$ is $0.171$, $0.171$, $0.163$ across rows whose
revolutions, switches and node counts each vary ninefold. It peaks at
\emph{apogee} by $16$--$19\times$, because the mesh is uniform in longitude and
apogee carries the longest time-steps. Switch cells carry $10$--$16\%$ of the
total residual against a $\sim\!10\%$ population share---mildly
over-represented, not dominant.

The solution satisfies the equations it was \emph{solved against} some
$7\times10^{11}$ times better than the continuous equations. That gap is
discretization error, visible without any ladder.

\subsection{What was withdrawn, and why it matters}

Two separate attempts to turn that into a number failed external review:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Richardson orders fitted \emph{across} a switch-topology change,
  violating the study's own stated policy.
  \item An error model $E = k\sum h^3$ extrapolated from two points, assuming a
  phase-independent coefficient that the same experiment had already falsified.
\end{enumerate}

Both times the contradicting evidence was already written down in the same
document. \textbf{The failure was never missing data; it was not applying our
own stated constraint to our own new claim.} What survives is a
\emph{lower bound}: two $10$~N solutions at identical node count differing only
in node placement have masses $0.1456$~kg apart.

\section{Unanswered questions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Is any production mesh adequate, quantitatively?} Unresolved
  for all four campaigns. A lower bound exists at earth $10$~N only.
  \item \textbf{What is the observed order of these transcriptions?} Unknown.
  Every attempt crossed a branch change.
  \item \textbf{Do the certified rows represent the best available optima?}
  Demonstrably \emph{no} for the three earth rows; unknown elsewhere; the tulip
  flagship survives a weak probe and has a known duplicate optimum at a
  different $t_f$.
  \item \textbf{Why do near-minimum-time transfers resist solution?} The
  $1.01$--$1.11\times$ band fails for the smooth energy problem too, so it is
  conditioning rather than bang-bang structure. Unexplained.
  \item \textbf{Can indirect methods be made to work at scale here?} The one
  serious attempt stalled on conditioning at $\|R\|\sim0.023$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Recommended next steps}
\label{sec:rec}

\subsection{The question posed: bank and go indirect, or something closer?}

Going straight to indirect methods for all four problems would repeat a known
failure. The tulip indirect campaign already stalled on terminal-cluster
conditioning, and nothing has changed that would make the same approach work on
three more problems. Indirect methods need good seeds and exact switch
structure; we currently produce neither reliably.

\subsection{Recommendation 1 (immediate, cheap): a basin sweep}

Every certified earth row is beaten on its own mesh by $1.0$ to $6.0$~kg, found
by perturbations as trivial as a one-node throttle shift. The tulip campaign
independently records two certified optima $1.43\%$ apart in $\dV$. The probe
costs three to fifteen minutes per row and already exists.

This is not a research question. It is an operational gap: ``certified'' means
converged, not best-found, and closing it improves published numbers directly.
The $2.5$~N row looks $\sim\!6$~kg pessimistic, which is $0.4\%$---well above
anything quotable as numerical noise.

\subsection{Recommendation 2 (strategic): fixed-structure multi-phase with
switch times as variables}

This is the item worth building, and it is worth more than it first appears
because it resolves four separate problems at once.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Branch identity becomes structural.} Both withdrawn results
  trace to being unable to prove two solutions are the same solution. With the
  switch sequence fixed by construction, they are the same by definition, and
  mesh convergence becomes measurable.
  \item \textbf{It supplies the switch-aligned mesh} whose order the study has
  been trying to infer indirectly. The toy validation already showed the
  mechanism: a true retained breakpoint is a \emph{duplicated} node, which is
  exactly what a phase boundary provides.
  \item \textbf{It is the bridge to indirect methods, not a detour.} Switch
  times as explicit decision variables \emph{is} the indirect structure---the
  same switching conditions the Pontryagin formulation imposes---but solved by
  a well-conditioned NLP instead of by shooting. It produces exactly the seeds
  the stalled indirect campaign lacked.
  \item \textbf{Every external reviewer independently pointed at it}, across
  two separate review rounds and two different questions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Recommendation 3 (after 2): indirect methods, seeded}

With exact switch times and well-conditioned multi-phase solutions in hand, the
indirect route becomes a finishing step rather than a cold start---which is
what the stalled campaign was attempting without them. Start with the earth
two-body problem, where the reference implementation exists for comparison.

\subsection{What not to do}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Do not chase the optimal mesh.} Node redistribution improves the
  smaller error term. The measured basin gains exceed any plausible mesh error.
  \item \textbf{Do not extend the mesh ladder.} It cannot hold a branch;
  six perturbations of the deeper rows all reorganized their switch structure,
  in both directions and at every magnitude tried.
  \item \textbf{Do not merge the tulip and ELFO solvers.} They share $76\%$ of
  their lines and differ in the part that matters---the two-primary clock and
  free final time. Extract the incidental duplication only.
\end{itemize}
